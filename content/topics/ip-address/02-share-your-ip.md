## Who needs it

Common reasons are a web host, supplier or payment provider that only allows connections from known addresses, a firewall rule, or technical support checking why a connection fails. Share it with them, not in a public post or review.

## It can change

Most home and small business internet plans have a **dynamic address**. It can change when your modem restarts or after a power outage. If a login or allow list that used to work stops working, look the addresses up again and send the new ones. Your internet provider can often sell you a static address that never changes.

## Mobile and shared connections

On mobile data, and with some internet providers, many customers share one public IPv4 address and it changes often. This is called carrier-grade NAT. An allow list built on that address may stop working without warning, and a static address may not be available. For a computer that has to stay allowed, use a wired business connection with a static address, or ask the service what else it accepts.

## Is it private

An IP address can show your approximate area and your internet provider, but not your name or street address. Still, treat it like a phone number: fine to give to a provider you trust, not something to post publicly.
