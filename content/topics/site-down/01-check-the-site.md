## Check your internet first

Open a different website you use often. If nothing loads, the problem is your internet connection, not the site. Restart your modem or router, or move closer to your Wi-Fi.

## Ask a checker

Click the **Open Down for Everyone or Just Me** button above, or go to downforeveryoneorjustme.com. Type the site's address and check it. The checker tries the site from its own servers, so **if it can reach the site, the problem is likely on your side**. For big services like banks, email or social media, the service's own status page or its social media posts often say when it's down.

## Try another connection

On your phone, turn off Wi-Fi and open the site on mobile data. If it works there, the problem is your home or office network. Work networks, VPNs and security software sometimes block sites. Turn off a VPN and try again, or ask your IT team.

## Read the error

The message on the page is a clue. "This site can't be reached" or "Server not found" often means a typo in the address or a network problem. "404" or "Page not found" means that page has moved, so try the site's home page. Errors starting with 5, like 500, 502 or 503, mean the site's server has a problem, and the fix is on their side.

## If it's only you

Try a different browser, or a private or incognito window. If that works, follow the Clear cache and cookies for one site guide, or the Turn off ad blockers or extensions for one site guide. If nothing helps, contact the site's support with the exact error, when it happened, and your browser and device. A screenshot of the error helps.
