## Ask once, then wait

Go to the site's sign-in page and click its **Forgot password** or **Reset password** link. Type your email address carefully and send the request once. **Reset emails can take up to 10 minutes.** Asking again and again can trigger a limit that blocks more emails for a while, and each new email usually makes the older links stop working.

## Look beyond your inbox

Check your **spam** or **junk** folder first. In Gmail, also look in the Promotions and Updates tabs, and in Outlook, the Other tab. Then search your whole mailbox for the site's or business's name, since the email may come from a name you don't expect. If you find it in spam, mark it as not spam so the next one lands in your inbox.

Work and school email can hold messages back for checking. If you use one of those addresses, ask your IT team whether the email was held.

## Check the address

Many people have more than one email address. Try each one you might have used to sign up, including old ones. Look for a welcome or order email from the site to see which address it knows. For privacy, many sites say an email was sent even when no account uses that address, so a "check your email" message doesn't prove the address is right.

## Use the newest link

When the email arrives, click the link in the **newest** one. Links expire, often within an hour, so use it soon. If you signed up by clicking "Continue with Google" or Apple, you may have no password to reset: use that same button to sign in.

## When to contact support

If nothing arrives after 15 minutes and you have checked spam and every address, contact the site's support. Tell them the email address you tried, roughly when you asked for the reset, and whether you found anything in spam. Never send them your old or new password.
