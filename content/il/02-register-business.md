## Sole proprietor: assumed name certificate

If you trade under a name other than your own, file an **assumed name certificate** (often called a DBA) with the **county clerk** in each county where you do business. It lists your name, your address and every business address, and it must be notarized. Each county sets its own fee: for example **$50 in Cook County and $10 in DuPage County (seen November 2026; confirm current fee)**. Cook County lets you apply online. Ask your own county clerk for the current fee and form.

Your filing is public and shows your business address. If you work from home, ask the clerk what address you can use.

## Newspaper publication

Illinois law requires you to **publish a notice in a newspaper in your county once a week for 3 weeks in a row**. The first notice must run within 15 days of filing, and the proof of publication must reach the clerk within 50 days, or the filing is void. The newspaper sets its own price. In Cook County, the clerk issues your certificate once it receives the proof.

If you change your name or addresses, or start doing business in another county, file again. Doing business under an unregistered assumed name is a misdemeanor under state law.

## LLC: Articles of Organization and annual report

If you chose an LLC in step 1, file Articles of Organization with the Illinois Secretary of State ($150, seen November 2026; confirm current fee). An LLC does not file with the county clerk. An LLC trading under another name files an assumed name with the Secretary of State instead, for up to $150 depending on the year (seen November 2026; confirm current fee).

Every year, the LLC files an **annual report ($75, seen November 2026; confirm current fee) before the first day of the month it was formed**. If it is late, the LLC falls out of good standing and a $100 penalty can apply.

## What a sole proprietor files each year

A sole proprietor has no yearly state filing for the business name. Ask your county clerk whether your certificate needs renewing, and ask an accountant about anything else that applies to you.

## Keep it

Save the certificate and the proof of publication. You will need them for the bank account (step 8) and your supplier account (step 13).
