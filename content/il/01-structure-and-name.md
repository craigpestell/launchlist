## Structure

Start as a **sole proprietorship**. There is no state filing to set one up, and the Illinois Department of Commerce says a sole proprietor's business name "does not involve the Secretary of State". You can form an LLC later. The trade-off is that you are personally liable for the business. Insurance (step 7) is how you manage that risk.

An LLC is formed by filing Articles of Organization with the Illinois Secretary of State. The Limited Liability Company Act sets the filing fee at **$150 (seen November 2026; confirm current fee)**. After that, the LLC files an annual report every year for $75 (seen November 2026; confirm current fee), see step 2. If you switch from a sole proprietorship to an LLC later, update your sales tax registration (step 4).

## Name

- If you trade under just your own legal name, you do not need to file a name.
- If you use any other name, for example "Jane's Gift Shop", you **must file an assumed name with your county clerk** and publish a newspaper notice in step 2.
- An LLC name must include "limited liability company", "L.L.C." or "LLC", must not include words like "Inc." or "Corp.", and must be different from names on the Secretary of State's records.

Assumed names for sole proprietors are filed county by county, so there is no single statewide list to search. Ask your county clerk how to check; Cook County, for example, has an online search.

## State income tax

**Illinois taxes personal income at a flat 4.95%** (seen November 2026; confirm current rate), including your profit as a sole proprietor or single-member LLC owner. You report it on your Illinois return, and there is no separate registration. The Department of Revenue says you must make estimated payments if you expect to owe more than $1,000 for the year, due April 15, June 15, September 15 and January 15. Illinois cities do not appear to add their own income tax (checked November 2026). Ask an accountant what applies to you. Federal tax is in step 9.

## Consumer privacy law

Illinois does not appear to have a broad consumer privacy law like some other states (checked November 2026; confirm with the Attorney General). The **Personal Information Protection Act applies to any business of any size** that holds Illinois residents' personal information: you must notify them after a data breach. If you ever collect fingerprints or face scans, for example through a virtual try-on tool, the Biometric Information Privacy Act requires written consent first. See step 11.
