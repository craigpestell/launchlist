## The Certificate of Registration

Illinois's sales tax is called the Retailers' Occupation Tax, and the permit is a **Certificate of Registration** from the Illinois Department of Revenue (IDOR). Register online through **MyTax Illinois**, which takes about 1–2 business days, or by paper Form REG-1, which takes weeks. IDOR says to register **before you make any purchases or sales**. This includes selling at craft shows and fairs. IDOR says there is no registration fee (seen November 2026; confirm when you apply). Certificates are no longer mailed: print yours from MyTax Illinois.

## The rate

The state rate is **6.25%** on general merchandise, and 1% on qualifying drugs and medical appliances. Counties, cities, transit districts and other local bodies add their own rates, which often change on January 1 and July 1. Use the Tax Rate Finder on MyTax Illinois to look up the rate for a location. The state 1% tax on groceries ended January 1, 2026, but some cities and counties charge their own. Ask an accountant how rates apply to what you sell.

## Filing

You file **Form ST-1**, and IDOR tells you how often. Its instructions say monthly if you average more than $200 tax a month, quarterly for $50–$200, and yearly under $50. Monthly returns are due the 20th of the next month, quarterly returns the 20th after the quarter, and yearly returns January 20. Many sellers must file online.

## Buying stock to resell

Give your suppliers a completed **Form CRT-61, Certificate of Resale**, so you do not pay sales tax on stock you resell. You need an active Illinois registration or resale number to use it. Do not use it for things you keep for the business. See step 13.

## Sellers outside Illinois

From January 1, 2026, out-of-state sellers must register once they have **$100,000 or more in sales to Illinois buyers** in the past 12 months (seen November 2026; confirm current threshold). The old 200-transaction test no longer applies. For selling into other states yourself, see step 6.
