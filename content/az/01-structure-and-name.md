## Structure

Start as a **sole proprietorship**. There is no state filing to set one up, and you can form an LLC later. The trade-off is that you are personally liable for the business. Insurance (step 7) is how you manage that risk.

An LLC is formed by filing Articles of Organization with the Arizona Corporation Commission (ACC). The fee is **$50, or $85 expedited (seen November 2026; confirm current fee)**. The ACC's FAQ quotes $85 for filing online. Arizona LLCs do not file an annual report, so there is no yearly state fee to keep one. You do need a statutory agent with an Arizona street address, and some new LLCs must publish a newspaper notice (see step 2). If you switch from a sole proprietorship to an LLC later, check with the Department of Revenue whether your sales tax license (step 4) needs updating.

## Name

- If you trade under just your own legal name, you do not need to register a name.
- If you use any other name, for example "Jane's Gift Shop", you can register it as a trade name with the Arizona Secretary of State. In Arizona this is **optional, not required**, but banks often ask for it (step 2).
- An LLC name must include "limited liability company", "limited company", "LLC", "L.L.C.", "LC" or "L.C." and must be distinguishable from names already on the ACC's and the Secretary of State's records.

The ACC's name availability search, in the Arizona Business Center, checks corporations, LLCs and registered trade names in one place.

## State income tax

**Arizona taxes personal income at a flat 2.5%** (seen November 2026; confirm the current rate with the Arizona Department of Revenue). That includes your profit as a sole proprietor or single-member LLC owner, reported on your Arizona return with no separate registration. You may need to pay Arizona estimated tax if your income is above the state's thresholds. No Arizona city income tax was found. Ask an accountant what applies to you. Federal tax is in step 9.

## Consumer privacy law

Arizona does not appear to have a broad consumer privacy law like some other states (checked November 2026; confirm with the Attorney General). Its **data breach law applies to any business** that holds Arizona residents' personal information on computer: you must notify affected people within 45 days of finding a breach, and tell the Attorney General if more than 1,000 people are affected. See step 11.
