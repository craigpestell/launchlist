## The TPT license

Arizona's sales tax is called the **transaction privilege tax (TPT)**. It is a tax on you, the seller, for the privilege of doing business, not a tax on the buyer. You need a TPT license from the Arizona Department of Revenue (ADOR) **before you start selling**. You can apply on AZTaxes.gov. The state fee is **$12 per location, and each city adds its own license fee of up to $50 (seen November 2026; confirm current fee)**. ADOR collects both, so one application covers state and city TPT.

## The rate

The state rate for retail sales is **5.6%**: 5% plus a 0.6% education tax. Counties and cities add their own TPT rates, so the combined rate depends on where the sale happens. Use ADOR's TPT rate lookup to find the rate for each place. Ask an accountant how the rules apply to what you sell.

## Filing and renewal

Returns are **monthly by default**, due on the 20th of the next month. Smaller sellers can file less often: quarterly if your yearly tax is about $2,000–$8,000, or once a year if it is under $2,000. You file and pay on AZTaxes.gov. The license runs for the calendar year. There is no state renewal fee, but city renewal fees are due each January.

## Buying stock to resell

Give your suppliers a completed **Form 5000A, Arizona Resale Certificate**, so you do not pay tax on stock you resell. You need a TPT license to use it. Do not use it for things you keep for the business. See step 13.

## Sellers outside Arizona

Out-of-state sellers must get a TPT license once they have **more than $100,000 of Arizona sales** in the current or previous calendar year, not counting sales through a marketplace like Etsy or Amazon (seen November 2026; confirm current threshold). For selling into other states yourself, see step 6.
