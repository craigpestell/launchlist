## Structure

Start as a **sole proprietorship**. There is no state filing to set one up, and you can form an LLC later. The trade-off is that you are personally liable for the business. Insurance (step 7) is how you manage that risk.

An LLC is formed by filing Articles of Organization with the Virginia State Corporation Commission (SCC). The filing fee is **$100 (seen November 2026; confirm current fee)**. After that, the LLC pays a $50 annual registration fee every year (see step 2). It must also have a registered agent with a Virginia street address, and that address is public. If you switch from a sole proprietorship to an LLC later, update your sales tax registration (step 4).

## Name

- If you trade under just your own legal name, you do not need to file a name.
- If you use any other name, for example "Jane's Gift Shop", you **must file a certificate of assumed or fictitious name** with the SCC in step 2.
- An LLC name must include "limited liability company", "limited company", "LLC", "L.L.C.", "LC" or "L.C." and must be distinguishable from active business names on the SCC's records. Some words, such as "bank" or "engineer", are restricted.

A fictitious name does not have to be unique and gives you no exclusive right to it. Others can file the same name.

## State income tax

**Virginia taxes personal income**, including your profit as a sole proprietor or single-member LLC owner. You report it on your Virginia return, and there is no separate registration. The rates run from 2% to 5.75%, with 5.75% on taxable income over $17,000 (2026 rates, seen November 2026). If you expect to owe more than $1,000 after withholding and credits, you make estimated payments, due May 1, June 15, September 15 and January 15. Virginia's cities and counties do not levy their own income tax. Ask an accountant what applies to you. Federal tax is in step 9.

## Consumer privacy law

The **Virginia Consumer Data Protection Act** applies only to businesses that handle the personal data of at least 100,000 Virginia consumers a year, or at least 25,000 and earn over half their revenue from selling personal data. Most small stores fall below this. Virginia's data breach law applies to every business that holds residents' personal information: after a breach, you must notify the Attorney General and the affected people. See step 11.
