## Structure

Start as a **sole proprietorship**. There is no formation filing, and you can form an LLC later. You still register with the state for taxes (step 2). The trade-off is that you are personally liable for the business. Insurance (step 7) is how you manage that risk.

An LLC is formed by filing a Certificate of Formation with the Division of Revenue and Enterprise Services. The fee is **$100 (seen November 2026; confirm current fee)**. You also need a registered agent with a New Jersey address, which can be you. After that, the LLC files an annual report every year for $75 (seen November 2026; confirm current fee). See step 2.

## Name

- If you trade under just your own legal name, you do not need to register a name.
- If a sole proprietor uses any other name, for example "Jane's Gift Shop", you **register a trade name with the county clerk** in step 2.
- An LLC name must be available on the state's records. Search it on the New Jersey business name search before you file, and check the naming rules shown when you form the LLC.

Trade names are kept county by county, so there is no single statewide list to search. Ask your county clerk how to check.

## State income tax

**New Jersey taxes personal income**, including your profit as a sole proprietor or single-member LLC owner, at graduated rates from 1.4% to 10.75% (seen November 2026; confirm current rates). You report it on your New Jersey resident return (NJ-1040). You must make estimated payments if you expect to owe more than $400 after withholding and credits. No city or local income tax was found. Ask an accountant what applies to you. Federal tax is in step 9.

## Consumer privacy law

New Jersey has a broad consumer privacy law, the **New Jersey Data Privacy Act**. It is reported to apply to businesses that handle the personal data of at least 100,000 New Jersey consumers a year, or at least 25,000 if they make money from selling personal data (checked November 2026; confirm on the Division of Consumer Affairs' FAQ). Most new small stores fall below that. New Jersey's data breach law applies to businesses of any size that hold residents' personal information. See step 11.
