## Bookkeeping

Use simple software such as Wave, QuickBooks or Xero. Keep **sales tax collected** separate from your income: it belongs to the state. Keep every receipt.

## Federal income tax

As a sole proprietor, or a single-member LLC that hasn't chosen otherwise, you report business profit on your personal return with **Schedule C**. You also pay **self-employment tax** of 15.3% on net earnings of $400 or more, on Schedule SE (seen November 2026). {region} may also have its own income tax: see {step:structure-and-name}.

## Quarterly estimated tax

No one withholds tax from business income. **If you expect to owe $1,000 or more, you pay estimated tax four times a year**, usually by April 15, June 15, September 15 and January 15. You pay with Form 1040-ES or online through your IRS account.

## Form 1099-K

Payment apps and marketplaces send you and the IRS a Form 1099-K once your payments pass a threshold. You must report all your income whether or not you get one.

## Records and an accountant

The IRS generally wants records kept **three years** after you file, and longer in some cases. A one-time consult early on is cheap compared with fixing mistakes later. Bring this checklist and ask about sales in other states.
