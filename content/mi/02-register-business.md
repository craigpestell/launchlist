## Sole proprietor: county clerk

If you trade under a name other than your own, file an **assumed name certificate with the county clerk** in each county where you do business or have an office, before you start trading. The county supplies the form, and you sign it in front of a notary. Fees are set county by county. The counties we checked charge **$10–$16 (seen November 2026; confirm with your county clerk)**, and the filing usually comes with certified copies.

The certificate lasts **five years**. The clerk mails a renewal notice before it expires, and renewing costs a few dollars. If you move the business, tell the clerk, and file again before trading in a new county. Your certificate is public and shows your full name and your home or business address.

Michigan's law has a narrow exception for selling by mail orders. Ask your county clerk before relying on it for an online store.

## LLC: Articles of Organization

If you chose an LLC in step 1, file Articles of Organization with the LARA Corporations Division, online or by mail. The fee is **$50 (seen November 2026; confirm current fee)**, and it is not refundable. If the LLC trades under a different name, it files a certificate of assumed name with LARA, not the county. That costs $25 and lasts until December 31 of the fifth full year after you file (seen November 2026; confirm current fee).

## LLC: annual statement

Every Michigan LLC files an **annual statement by February 15**, listing its resident agent and registered office. It costs $25 until September 30, 2027, and $15 after that (seen November 2026; confirm current fee). An LLC formed after September 30 skips the next February 15. Michigan has no separate franchise tax for a small LLC that we found. Ask an accountant how your LLC is taxed.

## What a sole proprietor files each year

A sole proprietor has **no yearly state filing for the business name**, only the renewal every five years. Your yearly filings are your sales tax returns (step 4) and your income tax returns (steps 1 and 9).
