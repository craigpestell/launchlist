## Structure

Start as a **sole proprietorship**. There is no state filing to set one up if you use your own name, and you can form an LLC later. The trade-off is that you are personally liable for the business. Insurance (step 7) is how you manage that risk.

An LLC is formed by filing Articles of Organization with the Corporations Division of the Michigan Department of Licensing and Regulatory Affairs (LARA). The filing fee is **$50 (seen November 2026; confirm current fee)**. After that, an LLC files an annual statement every year by February 15. It costs $25 until September 30, 2027, then $15 (seen November 2026; confirm current fee). See step 2. An LLC must also keep a registered office and a resident agent in Michigan.

## Name

- If you trade under just your own legal name, you do not need to file a name.
- If you use any other name, for example "Jane's Gift Shop", you **must file an assumed name certificate** with your county clerk in step 2.
- An LLC name must include "limited liability company", "L.L.C." or "L.C.". It cannot include "corporation" or "incorporated", and it must be different from names already on LARA's records.

Sole proprietors' assumed names are filed county by county, so there is no statewide list to search. The county clerk can turn down a name that is already filed in that county or could mislead people. Filing a name does not give you exclusive rights to it.

## State income tax

**Michigan taxes personal income at a flat rate**, 4.25% under the Income Tax Act. The law can lower the rate for a year, so check the current rate with the Michigan Department of Treasury (seen November 2026). Your profit as a sole proprietor or single-member LLC owner goes on your Michigan return, with no separate registration. If you expect to owe more than $500 of Michigan tax beyond any withholding, you pay estimated tax in four installments. About two dozen Michigan cities, including Detroit, also tax income where you live or work. Ask your city, and an accountant, what applies to you. Federal tax is in step 9.

## Consumer privacy law

Michigan **does not have a broad consumer privacy law** like some other states (a bill was still in the Senate when checked in November 2026). Michigan's **Identity Theft Protection Act does apply to any business that holds** Michigan residents' personal information: if a data breach is likely to cause loss or identity theft, you must notify the people affected. See step 11.
