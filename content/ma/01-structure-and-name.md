## Structure

Start as a **sole proprietorship**. There is no state filing to set one up, and you can form an LLC later. The trade-off is that you are personally liable for the business. Insurance (step 7) is how you manage that risk.

An LLC is formed by filing a certificate of organization with the Corporations Division of the Secretary of the Commonwealth. State law sets the filing fee at **$500, and every LLC then files an annual report that also costs $500 (seen November 2026; confirm current fees)**. The certificate names a resident agent in Massachusetts and becomes public record. See step 2.

## Name

- If you trade under just your own legal name, you do not need to file a name.
- If you use any other name, for example "Jane's Gift Shop", you **must file a business certificate** with your city or town clerk in step 2.
- An LLC name must include "Limited Liability Company", "Limited Company", "LLC", "L.L.C.", "LC" or "L.C." and must not be the same as, or confusingly close to, a business already on the Secretary's records.

Business certificates are kept town by town, so there is no single statewide list of sole proprietors' names. Ask your clerk how to search their records.

## State income tax

**Massachusetts taxes personal income**, including your profit as a sole proprietor or single-member LLC owner. Most income is taxed at a flat 5%, and income above about $1 million (adjusted each year for inflation) pays an extra 4% surtax (seen November 2026; confirm with the Department of Revenue). You report it on your Massachusetts return, and there is no separate registration. You must make estimated payments during the year if you expect to owe more than $400 in Massachusetts tax on income without withholding. We found no city or town income tax. Ask an accountant what applies to you. Federal tax is in step 9.

## Consumer privacy law

Massachusetts does not have a broad consumer privacy law in force. A Data Privacy Act passed both chambers in different versions and was in a conference committee (checked November 2026; confirm on the Legislature's site). The **data security law (Chapter 93H and its regulation 201 CMR 17.00) applies to any business of any size** that holds Massachusetts residents' personal information, such as a name with a card or account number. You need a written information security program sized to your business, and you must give notice after a data breach. See step 11.
