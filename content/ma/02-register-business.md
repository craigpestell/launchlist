## Sole proprietor: business certificate

If you trade under a name other than your own, Massachusetts law says you must file a **business certificate** (often called a DBA) with the **clerk of every city or town** where you have a business office. It lists your full name, your home address, the business address and the business name. You sign it under oath, in front of the clerk or a notary. You do not file it with the state, and there is no newspaper notice.

Each city or town sets its own fee. For example, Boston charges $65 and Plymouth $50 (seen November 2026; confirm with your clerk). Some towns ask for a zoning sign-off or a copy of your lease first, and Boston does not accept a PO box or virtual address. Your certificate is public, and you must show a copy to any customer who asks.

## Renew every four years

A business certificate **lasts four years and lapses unless you renew it**. Tell the clerk if you move, change your home address or stop trading. Doing business under a name without a certificate can mean a fine of up to $300 for each month.

## LLC: certificate of organization and annual report

If you chose an LLC in step 1, file a certificate of organization with the Corporations Division ($500, seen November 2026; confirm current fee). It names your Massachusetts office address and a resident agent. Then **file an annual report every year for $500**, due by the anniversary of the date you formed the LLC (confirm the due date with the Corporations Division). An LLC trading under any name other than its registered name also files a business certificate with the city or town clerk.

A sole proprietor has no yearly state filing for the business name, only the four-year renewal with the clerk. Ask an accountant about anything else that applies to you.

## Keep it

Save the filed certificate. You will need it for the bank account (step 8) and your supplier account (step 13).
