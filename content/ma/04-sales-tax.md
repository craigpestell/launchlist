## The registration

Massachusetts calls its permit a **Sales and Use Tax Registration**. It comes from the Massachusetts Department of Revenue (DOR), and you apply online through MassTaxConnect. State law says **you may not sell as a vendor without one**, and you need one for each place of business. This applies even if you sell from home, online or at a few craft fairs. DOR's pages read for this guide did not state a fee (seen November 2026; confirm when you apply). Post the certificate where customers can see it.

## The rate

The rate is **6.25%, the same everywhere in Massachusetts**. Cities and towns do not add sales tax on goods; their local options cover restaurant meals and lodging. Clothing and footwear are exempt up to $175 of the price of each item, but athletic and protective gear is taxed. Ask an accountant how the rules apply to what you sell.

## Filing

State law sets **monthly returns, due within 30 days after each period**, and lets DOR move smaller vendors to quarterly or annual filing. Check the schedule DOR assigns you in MassTaxConnect after you register. You file on MassTaxConnect, including for periods with no sales.

## Buying stock to resell

Give your suppliers a completed **Form ST-4, Sales Tax Resale Certificate**, with your registration number, so you do not pay sales tax on stock you resell. Do not use it for things you keep for the business. See step 13.

## Sellers outside Massachusetts

Out-of-state sellers must register once their **sales into Massachusetts exceed $100,000** in the current or prior year (seen November 2026; confirm current threshold). For selling into other states yourself, see step 6.
