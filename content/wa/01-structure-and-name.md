## Structure

Start as a **sole proprietorship**. There is no Secretary of State filing to set one up, and you can form an LLC later if the business grows. The trade-off is that you are personally liable for the business. Insurance (step 7) is how you manage that risk.

An LLC is formed by filing a Certificate of Formation with the Washington Secretary of State. The filing fee is $180 (seen November 2026; confirm current fee), plus an online processing fee if you file online. Every year after that, the LLC files an annual report, which costs $70 (seen November 2026; confirm current fee). Step 2 has the details.

## Name

- If you trade under your own full legal name, you do not need to register a name.
- If you use any other name, for example "Jane's Gift Shop", you **must register it as a trade name** with the Department of Revenue in the next step.
- An LLC name must include "Limited Liability Company", "Limited Liability Co.", "L.L.C." or "LLC", and must be different from names already on the Secretary of State's records. Some words, such as "bank" or "trust", are restricted.

Registering a trade name does not give you exclusive rights to it. For wider protection, you can also search the federal trademark records at the USPTO.

## State income tax

**Washington has no state income tax** on individuals, so there is nothing to register for income tax with the state and no state estimated payments. You still pay federal income tax (step 9). A new Washington tax on personal income over $1 million starts in 2028, which is unlikely to affect a new small shop. Washington does have a business and occupation (B&O) tax on gross receipts instead (step 4). Ask an accountant which structure suits you.

## Consumer privacy law

Washington has **no comprehensive consumer privacy law** (checked November 2026). Its **My Health My Data Act** applies to businesses of any size that collect consumer health data, which can include data that suggests a customer's health from what they buy. If it applies, you need a separate health data privacy policy linked from your homepage. Washington's data breach law applies to every business that holds personal information about Washington residents. Check the Attorney General's page and step 11.
