## Structure

Start as a **sole proprietorship**. There is no state filing to set one up, and you can form an LLC later. The trade-off is that you are personally liable for the business. Insurance (step 7) is how you manage that risk.

An LLC is formed by filing Articles of Organization with the Tennessee Secretary of State. The fee is $50 per member, with a **$300 minimum (seen November 2026; confirm current fee)**, so a single-member LLC pays $300. After that, an LLC pays at least $300 a year for its annual report and at least $100 a year in franchise tax, even with one owner and no profit (see step 2). A sole proprietor pays neither.

## Name

- A sole proprietor is registered under your own legal name. Tennessee has no state DBA filing for sole proprietors. You add a trade name, for example "Jane's Gift Shop", when you register for sales tax (step 4) and your business license (step 5).
- An LLC name must include "Limited Liability Company", "LLC" or "L.L.C." and must be different from names already on the Secretary of State's records. Words such as "bank" or "trust" need extra approval.
- An LLC that trades under another name files an assumed name with the Secretary of State (step 2).

The Secretary of State's name search covers LLCs and corporations only, so ask your county clerk whether another local business already uses your trade name.

## State income tax

**Tennessee has no income tax on wages or business income** for individuals, so your profit as a sole proprietor is not taxed by the state and there is no state estimated tax to pay. The old Hall tax on interest and dividends ended in 2021. No city or county income tax was found (checked November 2026). An LLC is different: it pays franchise and excise tax (step 2). Federal tax is in step 9, and an accountant can tell you what applies to you.

## Consumer privacy law

The **Tennessee Information Protection Act** took effect on July 1, 2025, but it only covers businesses with more than **$25 million** in revenue that also handle data on large numbers of Tennessee consumers (175,000 a year, or 25,000 if most revenue comes from selling data). A small online store will not meet it. Tennessee's data breach law does apply to any business that holds people's personal information on computer: you must notify affected people after a breach. See step 11.
