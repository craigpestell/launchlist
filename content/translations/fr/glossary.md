# French (Canada) glossary

Terms the French guides must use, so every guide says the same thing the same way. Use the official French name a government body uses for itself and its programs; if a body has no official French name (many provincial registries outside Quebec, New Brunswick and Ontario), keep the English name and add a French gloss the first time: "BC Registry (registre des entreprises de la Colombie-Britannique)".

Wording and typography rules for every language live in the `writing-style` skill (`.claude/skills/writing-style/SKILL.md`) once it lands; until then the short version is below. Add a term here whenever you settle on one.

## Style

Wording and typography rules are in the `writing-style` skill (`.claude/skills/writing-style/SKILL.md`, section "Canadian French (fr-CA)"). In short: vous; titles, headings and checklist items in the infinitive ("S'inscrire à la TPS (5 %)"); body text in the imperative; Canadian spacing (non-breaking space before ":" only, and before "%" and "$"), which `npm run translations -- stamp` applies.

Rules specific to translations:

- Keep `{step:<slug>}` and `{region}` tokens exactly; use `{in-region}` where French needs the region with its preposition ("au Québec", "en Ontario"), never "en {region}".
- `linkLabel` keeps the English pattern "Organisation — page" with an em dash: "ARC — TPS/TVH pour les entreprises".
- Brand and product names stay as they are. Buttons and menus on a site with no French interface stay in English in « », with a French gloss in brackets the first time: « Add to cart » (ajouter au panier). Use a French interface label only when you are sure of it.
- A government body or law with no official French name keeps its English name, with a French gloss the first time: "BC Registry (registre des entreprises de la Colombie-Britannique)".
