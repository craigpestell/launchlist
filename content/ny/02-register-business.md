## Sole proprietor: assumed name certificate

If you trade under a name other than your own, file an **assumed name certificate** (often called a DBA) with the **county clerk** in each county where you do business. The form must be notarized. Business Express says you can buy it at a legal stationery store, and some county clerks let you file online. The fee is **$25 in most counties and $100 in the five New York City counties (seen November 2026; confirm current fee)**. Certified copies cost $4, or $10 in New York City.

Your filing is public and shows your business address. If you work from home, ask the clerk what address you can use.

## LLC: Articles of Organization and publication

If you chose an LLC in step 1, file Articles of Organization with the Department of State online or by mail ($200, seen November 2026; confirm current fee). Then, **within 120 days, publish a notice in two newspapers** chosen by your county clerk, once a week for six weeks, and file a Certificate of Publication ($50). If you skip this, the LLC loses its authority to do business in New York until you fix it. An LLC trading under another name also files a Certificate of Assumed Name with the Department of State ($25).

## LLC: yearly and two-yearly filings

- **Biennial statement:** $9 every two years, due in the month the LLC was formed (seen November 2026; confirm current fee). File it online with the Department of State, and not early.
- **IT-204-LL filing fee:** a single-member LLC with New York income pays $25 a year to the Tax Department, due March 15 for a calendar year (seen November 2026; confirm current fee).

A sole proprietor has no yearly state filing for the business name. Ask an accountant about anything else that applies to you.

## Keep it

Save the filed certificate and a certified copy. You will need them for the bank account (step 8) and your supplier account (step 13).
