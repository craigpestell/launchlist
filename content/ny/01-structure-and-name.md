## Structure

Start as a **sole proprietorship**. There is no state filing to set one up if you use your own name, and you can form an LLC later. The trade-off is that you are personally liable for the business. Insurance (step 7) is how you manage that risk.

An LLC is formed by filing Articles of Organization with the New York Department of State. The filing fee is **$200 (seen November 2026; confirm current fee)**. New York also requires a new LLC to publish a notice in two local newspapers for six weeks within 120 days, then file a Certificate of Publication ($50, seen November 2026; confirm current fee). The newspapers set their own prices, which vary a lot by county. After that, a single-member LLC pays a $25 yearly filing fee to the Tax Department and a $9 biennial statement every two years (see step 2). If you switch from a sole proprietorship to an LLC later, you need a new Certificate of Authority for sales tax (step 4).

## Name

- If you trade under just your own legal name, you do not need to file a name.
- If you use any other name, for example "Jane's Gift Shop", you **must file an assumed name certificate** in step 2.
- An LLC name must include "Limited Liability Company", "LLC" or "L.L.C." and must be different from names already on the Department of State's records. Some words are restricted.

Assumed names for sole proprietors are filed county by county, so there is no single statewide list to search. Ask your county clerk how to check.

## State income tax

**New York State taxes personal income**, including your profit as a sole proprietor or single-member LLC owner. You report it on your New York return, and there is no separate registration. You may need to pay New York estimated tax during the year. If you live in New York City or Yonkers, those cities also tax residents' income through the same return, and New York City has its own unincorporated business tax. Ask an accountant what applies to you. Federal tax is in step 9.

## Consumer privacy law

New York does not appear to have a broad consumer privacy law like some other states (checked November 2026; confirm with the Attorney General). The **SHIELD Act does apply to any business of any size** that holds New York residents' private information: you must keep it reasonably secure and give notice after a data breach. See the New York Attorney General's SHIELD Act page and step 11.
