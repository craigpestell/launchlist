## Sole proprietor: trade name registration

If you trade under a name that does not show your own name, Georgia law says you file a **trade name registration with the clerk of superior court** in the county where you mainly do business. The statement must be signed in front of a notary. Some clerks say to file before you start trading, others within 30 days of starting, so ask yours and file early. Some counties take filings only online through eFileGA.

Fees are set by each county. Gwinnett, DeKalb and Thomas County clerks list **about $172–$175 (seen November 2026; confirm current fee)**. Doing business under an unregistered trade name is a misdemeanor. Registering does not give you exclusive rights to the name.

## Newspaper publication

After filing, you publish a notice in the **county's legal newspaper (the legal organ) once a week for two weeks**. The newspaper sets the price. Gwinnett collected a $40 publication fee for its paper, and other counties have you pay the newspaper directly (seen November 2026; confirm with your clerk). Keep the proof of publication.

## LLC: Articles of Organization and annual registration

If you chose an LLC in step 1, file Articles of Organization with the Secretary of State ($100 online or $110 by mail, seen November 2026; confirm current fee). Then file an **annual registration every year between January 1 and April 1**, starting the year after you form it. The Secretary of State's fee schedule lists $50 plus a $10 service charge, and a $25 late fee after April 1 (seen November 2026; confirm current fee). You can pay for two or three years at once. If you skip it, the LLC can be dissolved.

A sole proprietor has no yearly state filing for the business or the trade name. Ask an accountant about anything else that applies to you.

## Keep it

Save the filed registration and the proof of publication. You will need them for the bank account (step 8) and your supplier account (step 13).
