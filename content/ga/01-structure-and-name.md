## Structure

Start as a **sole proprietorship**. Georgia.gov says sole proprietorships are not registered with the Secretary of State, and you can form an LLC later. The trade-off is that you are personally liable for the business. Insurance (step 7) is how you manage that risk.

An LLC is formed by filing Articles of Organization with the Georgia Secretary of State, Corporations Division. Georgia.gov lists the filing fee as **$100 online or $110 by mail (seen November 2026; confirm current fee)**. Online filings take about 7 business days. After that, an LLC files an annual registration every year by April 1 (see step 2). An LLC also needs a registered agent with an address in Georgia.

## Name

- If you trade under just your own legal name, you do not need to file a name.
- If you use any other name, for example "Jane's Gift Shop", you **must register it as a trade name** with the clerk of superior court in your county (step 2).
- An LLC name must show that it is an LLC and must be available on the Secretary of State's records. Check the Secretary of State's name rules before you file.

You can reserve an LLC name for 30 days with the Secretary of State ($25 online, seen November 2026; confirm current fee). Trade names for sole proprietors are kept by each county clerk, so ask your county's clerk how to search them.

## State income tax

**Georgia taxes personal income at a flat 4.99% for 2026** (seen November 2026; confirm the current rate with the Department of Revenue). Your profit as a sole proprietor or single-member LLC owner goes on your Georgia return, and there is no separate registration. If enough of your income has no tax withheld, you may need to pay Georgia estimated tax during the year with Form 500-ES. Ask an accountant what applies to you. Federal tax is in step 9.

## Consumer privacy law

Georgia **does not appear to have a broad consumer privacy law** like some other states (checked November 2026; confirm with the Attorney General). Georgia's data breach notice law is written for certain kinds of businesses, so ask an accountant or attorney whether it covers yours. Keep customer data secure either way. See step 11.
