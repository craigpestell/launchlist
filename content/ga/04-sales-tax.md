## The certificate of registration

Georgia's sales tax permit is a **sales and use tax number with a certificate of registration**. It comes from the Georgia Department of Revenue, and you apply online through the Georgia Tax Center. The Department says anyone who meets the definition of a dealer must register, even if every sale is online, out of state or wholesale. A sole proprietor registers as an individual. The Department says your account number usually arrives by email within 15 minutes, and the registration does not need renewing. No registration fee for sales tax is listed (seen November 2026; confirm when you apply). Display the certificate where you do business.

## The rate

The state rate is **4%**. Counties add local taxes, and the City of Atlanta adds its own. Combined rates in the Department's chart run from about 6% to 9% (seen November 2026; the chart is updated each quarter). You generally charge the rate for the county where the customer takes delivery, even if you have no presence there. Ask an accountant how rates apply to what you sell.

## Filing

The Department says **most sellers file a return every month**, due by the 20th of the following month. You file even if you made no sales. You must file online if you owe more than $500 on a return. You can ask the Department in writing about a different filing schedule.

## Buying stock to resell

Give your suppliers a completed **Form ST-5, Sales Tax Certificate of Exemption**, so you do not pay sales tax on stock you resell. Do not use it for things you keep for the business. See step 13.

## Sellers outside Georgia

Out-of-state sellers must register once they have **more than 200 separate sales into Georgia or at least $100,000 in Georgia sales** in the previous calendar year (seen November 2026; confirm current threshold). If you sell only through a marketplace, the marketplace may collect for you. For selling into other states yourself, see step 6.
