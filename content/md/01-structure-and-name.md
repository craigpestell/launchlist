## Structure

Start as a **sole proprietorship**. You can form an LLC later. The trade-off is that you are personally liable for the business. Insurance (step 7) is how you manage that risk. Even a sole proprietor gets an identification number from the State Department of Assessments and Taxation (SDAT) in step 2.

An LLC is formed by filing Articles of Organization with SDAT. The filing fee is **$100 (seen November 2026; confirm current fee)**, and standard processing takes 6–8 weeks online. Faster service costs extra. After that, every LLC files an Annual Report with SDAT by April 15 with a **$300 fee** (seen November 2026; confirm current fee). An LLC must also name a resident agent with a Maryland address. See step 2.

## Name

- If you trade under just your own legal name, you do not need to file a name.
- If you use any other name, for example "Jane's Gift Shop", you **must file a trade name with SDAT before you start**. See step 2.
- An LLC name must include "Limited Liability Company", "LLC", "L.L.C.", "LC" or "L.C.".

Use the name search on Maryland Business Express to see whether a name is already in use. A trade name filing gives you no extra rights to the name, so check trademarks too if the name matters to you.

## State income tax

**Maryland taxes personal income**, including your profit as a sole proprietor or single-member LLC owner. State rates run from 2% to 6.5% in brackets. On top of that, **every county and Baltimore City adds a local income tax of 2.25%–3.30%**, collected on the same state return. Maryland also has quarterly estimated tax payments. Ask an accountant what applies to you. Federal tax is in step 9.

## Consumer privacy law

Maryland has a comprehensive privacy law, the **Maryland Online Data Privacy Act**. It applies to a business that, in the past calendar year, handled the personal data of **at least 35,000 consumers** (not counting data used only to complete a payment), or at least 10,000 consumers while earning more than 20% of its revenue from selling personal data. Most small stores start well below that. Maryland's breach law applies to **every business** holding Maryland residents' personal information: you must notify affected people within 45 days of finding a breach that is likely to lead to misuse. See step 11.
