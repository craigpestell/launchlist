## Trader's license

Maryland requires a **trader's license** for anyone who runs a place of business selling goods, with a separate license for each location. It comes from the **Clerk of the Circuit Court** in your county, not from a city office. People who make the goods they sell are exempt. Whether a home-based online store counts depends on your setup, so ask the clerk.

## The fee and the yearly cycle

The fee depends on your county and, in most counties, on the value of your stock. It starts at **$15 a year, or $20 in Baltimore City and Baltimore County** (seen November 2026; confirm current fee), and rises with stock value. Licenses run from May 1 to April 30, so you renew every spring. The clerk may need SDAT's figure for your stock from the yearly property return in step 2. Some counties, including Baltimore County and Howard County, need zoning approval first.

## County and city licenses

Maryland Business Express says some areas require a **local business license**, and rules differ by industry and location. Contact your county's inspections, licensing or permits office, and ask about home business and zoning rules if you work from home. Some products and trades need a state license, which you find on the Maryland OneStop portal.

## Markets and shows

If you sell at temporary spots, Maryland Business Express lists a **transient vendor license** from the Comptroller. At a flea market or show, you need a trader's license unless the show or your situation is exempt. Ask the show organizer what they need from you.

## Keep them

Save copies of every license and note the renewal dates. Your insurer (step 7) may ask for them.
