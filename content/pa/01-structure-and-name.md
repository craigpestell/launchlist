## Structure

Start as a **sole proprietorship**. There is no state filing to set one up if you use your own name, and you can form an LLC later. The trade-off is that you are personally liable for the business. Insurance (step 7) is how you manage that risk.

An LLC is formed by filing a Certificate of Organization with the Pennsylvania Department of State. The filing fee is **$125 (seen November 2026; confirm current fee)**, and no newspaper ad is needed for an LLC. After that, an LLC files an annual report each year for $7 (see step 2). An LLC also needs a Pennsylvania street address for its registered office, or a commercial registered office provider. If you switch from a sole proprietorship to an LLC later, check how to update your sales tax license (step 4).

## Name

- If you trade under just your own legal name, you do not need to register a name.
- If you use any other name, for example "Jane's Gift Shop", you **must register it as a fictitious name** with the Department of State in step 2. Adding words like "& Sons" also counts.
- A sole proprietor's name cannot include "Inc", "Corporation", "Limited" or "LLC". "Company" is allowed.
- An LLC name must include "Company", "Limited" or "Limited Liability Company", or an abbreviation, and must be different from names already on the Department of State's records.

Registering a fictitious name does not give you exclusive rights to it. Search the Department of State's business records before you decide.

## State income tax

**Pennsylvania taxes personal income at a flat 3.07%** (seen November 2026; confirm current rate), including your profit as a sole proprietor or single-member LLC owner. You report it on your Pennsylvania return. You may need to pay state estimated tax during the year if your income without withholding is high enough. Almost every Pennsylvania municipality also has a local earned income tax that covers business profits, and rates depend on where you live. Philadelphia has its own business taxes as well (see step 5). Ask an accountant what applies to you. Federal tax is in step 9.

## Consumer privacy law

Pennsylvania does not have a broad consumer privacy law in force. A bill was still in the legislature when checked (November 2026; confirm on the General Assembly's site). Pennsylvania does have a **data breach notification law** that applies to businesses holding residents' personal information, so keep customer data secure and know what to do if it leaks. See step 11 and ask an accountant or lawyer if you are unsure.
