## Sole proprietor: fictitious name

If you trade under a name other than your own, register it as a **fictitious name with the Pennsylvania Department of State**, not your county. You can file online through the Department's Business Filing Services. The fee is **$70 (seen November 2026; confirm current fee)**. The form asks for the name, what the business does, a street address for the business (a PO box alone is not accepted), and your name and address. Your registration is public.

If you do business under an unregistered name, you cannot enforce a contract made under that name in a Pennsylvania court until you register, and a court can add a $500 penalty.

## The newspaper notice

When an individual is listed as an owner, you must **advertise the name in two newspapers in the county** where the business is located, and one of them must be a legal newspaper. If only one newspaper circulates there, one is enough. The notice can say you have filed or intend to file. The Department publishes a county-by-county list of legal newspapers. You do not send proof to the Department, but keep it. The newspapers set their own prices, so ask them before you place the ads.

## LLC: Certificate of Organization

If you chose an LLC in step 1, file a Certificate of Organization with a docketing statement ($125, seen November 2026; confirm current fee). No newspaper ad is needed to form an LLC. An LLC trading under another name also registers a fictitious name ($70).

## LLC: yearly annual report

An LLC files an **annual report between January 1 and September 30** each year. The fee is $7 (seen November 2026; confirm current fee), and you file it online with the Department of State. Beginning with reports due in 2027, an LLC that does not file can be dissolved and lose its name. Watch out for companies that charge much more to file it for you.

## Sole proprietors

A sole proprietor files no yearly state report for a fictitious name. Save the filed registration and proof of publication. You will need them for the bank account (step 8) and your supplier account (step 13). Ask an accountant about anything else that applies to you.
