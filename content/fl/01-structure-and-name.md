## Structure

Start as a **sole proprietorship**. There is no state filing to set one up, and you can form an LLC later if the business grows. The trade-off is that you are personally liable for the business. Insurance (step 7) is how you manage that risk.

An LLC is formed by filing Articles of Organization with the Florida Division of Corporations, known as Sunbiz. The fee is $125, which covers the articles and the registered agent designation (seen November 2026; confirm current fee). An LLC then files an annual report every year between January 1 and May 1, which costs $138.75 (seen November 2026; confirm current fee). See step 2.

## Name

- If you trade under just your own legal name, you do not need to file a name.
- If you use any other name, for example "Jane's Gift Shop", you **must register it as a fictitious name** in the next step.
- An LLC name must include "Limited Liability Company", "LLC" or "L.L.C." and must be distinguishable from names already on the Division of Corporations' records. Adding "the", "and" or a different ending such as "Inc." does not make a name different.
- A fictitious name may not include "Inc.", "LLC" or similar words unless your business really is that kind of entity.

Registering a fictitious name is public notice only. It does not reserve the name or stop someone else using it.

## State income tax

**Florida has no personal income tax**, so a sole proprietor has nothing to register for income tax with the state. You still pay federal income tax (step 9). A single-member LLC that is taxed like a sole proprietorship does not file a Florida corporate income tax return either. An LLC that chooses to be taxed as a corporation does, at 5.5% (seen November 2026; confirm current rate). Ask an accountant which structure suits you.

## Consumer privacy law

The main duties of the **Florida Digital Bill of Rights apply only to businesses with more than $1 billion in yearly revenue**, so a small store won't meet them. Two rules still apply to you. If you ever sell customers' sensitive personal data, Florida requires their consent and a notice on your website. And Florida's data breach law requires every business to protect customers' personal information and tell them within 30 days of finding a breach. See step 11.
