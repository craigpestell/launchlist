## Sole proprietor: register of deeds

If you trade under a name other than your own, file an **assumed business name certificate with the register of deeds** in a county where you do business. One filing can cover several counties, or all 100, for the same fee. There is no notary requirement, and the certificate does not expire. County register of deeds offices list a recording fee of **$26 (seen November 2026; confirm current fee with your county)**. Some counties let you file online for an extra convenience fee.

The certificate is a public record and must give the street address of your principal place of business. A PO box is not accepted. If you work from home, think about which address you use. If the name, address or counties change, you must update the filing within 60 days.

## LLC: Secretary of State

If you chose an LLC in step 1, file Articles of Organization with the North Carolina Secretary of State, online or by mail. The fee is **$125 (seen November 2026; confirm current fee)**. You must name a registered agent, who can be you if you live in North Carolina, with a street address in the state. That address is on the public record. An LLC that trades under another name files an assumed business name certificate with the register of deeds, the same way a sole proprietor does.

## LLC: annual report

Every North Carolina LLC files an **annual report by April 15 each year**, starting the year after it is formed, even if it did no business. The fee is $200, plus a small card or bank fee if you file online (seen November 2026; confirm current fee). Missing it can lead to the LLC being dissolved. The Secretary of State warns about companies that send official-looking letters offering to file it for an extra charge. File it yourself on the Secretary of State's site.

## What a sole proprietor files each year

A sole proprietor has **no yearly state filing for the business name**. You file your sales tax returns (step 4) and your North Carolina income tax return. Corporations pay a state franchise tax, but a single-member LLC taxed like a sole proprietorship is not listed as subject to it. Ask an accountant to confirm what applies to you.
