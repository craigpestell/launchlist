## Sole proprietorship: county clerk

If you trade under a name other than your own, file an **assumed name certificate with the county clerk** in each county where you keep a business office. Fees are set by each county, so check with your county clerk. A certificate lasts up to 10 years.

## LLC: Secretary of State

You form an LLC by filing a certificate of formation (Form 205) with the Texas Secretary of State, online through SOSPortal or by mail. The fee is **$300 (seen November 2026; confirm current fee)**. You must name a registered agent with a physical Texas street address. If you act as your own agent, that address becomes public.

If the LLC trades under a name other than its legal name, it files an assumed name certificate (Form 503) with the Secretary of State, not the county. The fee is $25 (seen November 2026; confirm current fee).

## Yearly reports for an LLC

A Texas LLC does not file an annual report with the Secretary of State. Instead it files with the **Texas Comptroller by May 15 each year**, starting the year after it is formed.

- Below the no-tax-due threshold ($2,650,000 in total revenue for 2026 and 2027 reports, seen November 2026; confirm current figure), no franchise tax is owed, but you still file an information report.
- You also file a Public Information Report every year. There is no fee, but late reports carry a $50 penalty (seen November 2026; confirm current amount).

Ask an accountant to confirm what your LLC files. The Comptroller warns of scam texts claiming these reports are past due, so check directly on the Comptroller's website.

## Sole proprietors and franchise tax

Sole proprietorships are **not subject to the Texas franchise tax**, so you have no yearly state report beyond your sales tax returns (step 4).
