## Structure

Start as a **sole proprietorship**. There is no state filing to set one up, and you can form an LLC later if the business grows. The trade-off is that you are personally liable for the business. Insurance (step 7) is how you manage that risk.

An LLC is formed by filing a certificate of formation with the Texas Secretary of State. The filing fee is $300 (seen November 2026; confirm current fee), plus a small card fee if you file online. There is no yearly fee to the Secretary of State, but an LLC must file reports with the Texas Comptroller every year by May 15 (see step 2). If you switch from a sole proprietorship to an LLC later, you will need a new sales tax permit.

## Name

- If you trade under just your own legal name, you do not need to file a name.
- If you use any other name, for example "Jane's Gift Shop", you **must file an assumed name certificate** in the next step.
- An LLC name must be different from names already on the Secretary of State's records and must say it is an LLC. Check the exact wording rules in the certificate of formation instructions.

A name search on SOSPortal costs $1 (seen November 2026; confirm current fee). Filing an assumed name does not give you exclusive rights to it.

## State income tax

**Texas has no state income tax**, so there is nothing to register for income tax with the state. You still pay federal income tax (step 9). An LLC does not pay income tax to Texas either, but it is covered by the Texas franchise tax and has yearly reports to file (step 2). Ask an accountant which structure suits you.

## Consumer privacy law

The **Texas Data Privacy and Security Act** applies to many businesses that handle Texans' personal data, with no revenue threshold. Small businesses, as the US Small Business Administration defines them, are generally exempt, but even a small business must get consent before selling sensitive data. Check the Texas Attorney General's page and step 11.
