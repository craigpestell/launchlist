## Structure

Start as a **sole proprietorship**. The Indiana Department of Revenue says sole proprietorships are not required to register with the Indiana Secretary of State, and you can form an LLC later. The trade-off is that you are personally liable for the business. Insurance (step 7) is how you manage that risk.

An LLC is formed by filing Articles of Organization with the Indiana Secretary of State through INBiz, the state business portal. The fee is **$100 by mail, or about $95 online (seen November 2026; confirm the current fee on INBiz)**. After that, an LLC files a business entity report every two years ($31 online or $50 by mail, seen November 2026; confirm current fee). See step 2. If you switch from a sole proprietorship to an LLC later, you need a new sales tax registration and pay its $25 fee again (step 4).

## Name

- If your business name includes your own surname, for example "Smith's Candles" for Jane Smith, **Indiana does not require an assumed name filing**.
- If you use any other name, for example "Lakeside Candles", you must file a certificate of assumed business name with your county recorder in step 2.
- An LLC name must include "Limited Liability Company", "LLC" or "L.L.C." and must be different from names already on the Secretary of State's records.

Sole proprietors' assumed names are filed county by county, so there is no single statewide list. The Secretary of State's business search covers LLCs and corporations.

## State income tax

**Indiana taxes personal income at 2.95% for 2026**, falling to 2.90% in 2027 (seen November 2026; confirm current rate). That includes your profit as a sole proprietor or single-member LLC owner, reported on Form IT-40. Every Indiana county also has its own income tax, collected on the same return, and the rate depends on your county. If you expect to owe $1,000 or more in state and county tax for the year, you generally pay estimated tax in four installments. Ask an accountant what applies to you. Federal tax is in step 9.

## Consumer privacy law

Indiana's **Consumer Data Protection Act took effect on January 1, 2026**. According to the Attorney General, it applies to businesses that sell to Indiana residents and handle the personal data of at least 100,000 Indiana residents a year, or at least 25,000 if more than half their revenue comes from selling personal data (seen November 2026; confirm with the Attorney General). Most small stores fall below this. Indiana's breach notice law still applies to any business that holds customers' personal information: if it is exposed in a breach, you must tell the affected customers without unreasonable delay. See step 11.
