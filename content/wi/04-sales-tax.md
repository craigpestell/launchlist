## The seller's permit

Wisconsin's sales tax permit is called a **seller's permit**. It comes from the Wisconsin Department of Revenue (DOR). Apply online through Business Tax Registration, or on paper Form BTR-101, **at least three weeks before you open**. Online, DOR emails your permit number in 1–2 business days. The registration fee is **$20 for two years, then $10 for each further two years (seen November 2026; confirm current fee)**. DOR may ask some applicants for a security deposit of up to $15,000, often where there are unpaid taxes in the past. Display the permit where you sell. If you sell only through a marketplace that collects the tax for you, you may not need one; check with DOR.

## The rate

The state rate is **5%**. Most counties add 0.5%, Milwaukee County adds 0.9%, and a few counties add nothing. The city of Milwaukee adds 2%, so the total there is 7.9% (seen November 2026; confirm current rates). Some resort towns, such as Wisconsin Dells, add a premier resort area tax for certain business types. The rate depends on where the sale is sourced, usually where the buyer receives the goods, so use DOR's rate lookup. Ask an accountant how rates apply to what you sell.

## Filing

Most new sellers **file quarterly**, unless DOR tells you to file monthly or yearly. Returns are due the last day of the month after each period. You file a return even if you made no sales, and you must file online, for example through My Tax Account.

## Buying stock to resell

Give your suppliers a completed **Form S-211, Wisconsin Sales and Use Tax Exemption Certificate**, so you do not pay sales tax on stock you resell. Do not use it for things you keep for the business. See step 13.

## Sellers outside Wisconsin

Out-of-state sellers must register once their **gross sales into Wisconsin exceed $100,000** in the previous or current calendar year (seen November 2026; confirm current threshold). There is no transaction count test. For selling into other states yourself, see step 6.
