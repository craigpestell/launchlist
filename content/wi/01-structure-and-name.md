## Structure

Start as a **sole proprietorship**. There is no state filing to set one up, and you can form an LLC later. The trade-off is that you are personally liable for the business. Insurance (step 7) is how you manage that risk.

An LLC is formed by filing Articles of Organization with the Wisconsin Department of Financial Institutions (DFI). The filing fee is **$130 online or $170 on paper (seen November 2026; confirm current fee)**. After that, an LLC files an annual report each year, $25 online or $40 on paper (seen November 2026; confirm current fee), see step 2. An LLC also needs a registered agent in Wisconsin. If you switch from a sole proprietorship to an LLC later, you need a new seller's permit (step 4).

## Name

- If you trade under your own legal name, you do not need to file a name.
- If you use another name, for example "Jane's Gift Shop", Wisconsin **does not require you to register it**. You can choose to register it with DFI as a trade name in step 2.
- An LLC name must include "limited liability company", "limited company", "LLC" or "LC" and must be distinguishable from names already on DFI's records.

A trade name registration does not reserve an LLC name. Use DFI's free business entity search to check LLC names.

## State income tax

**Wisconsin taxes personal income**, including your profit as a sole proprietor or single-member LLC owner, at rates from 3.5% to 7.65% (seen November 2026; confirm current rates). You report it on your Wisconsin return, and there is no separate registration. If you expect to owe $500 or more when you file, you may need to make estimated payments on Form 1-ES. No city or county income tax was found in Wisconsin. Ask an accountant what applies to you. Federal tax is in step 9.

## Consumer privacy law

**Wisconsin does not have a broad consumer privacy law** (checked November 2026). A bill for one failed to pass in March 2026. Wisconsin's data breach law does apply to businesses, other than individuals, that hold Wisconsin residents' personal information: they must give notice within 45 days of learning of a breach. If you form an LLC, this covers you. Ask an accountant or lawyer whether it covers you as a sole proprietor. See step 11.
