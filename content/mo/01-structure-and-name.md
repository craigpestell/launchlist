## Structure

Start as a **sole proprietorship**. The Secretary of State says sole proprietorships are created without any filing with its office, and you can form an LLC later. The trade-off is that you are personally liable for the business. Insurance (step 7) is how you manage that risk.

An LLC is formed by filing Articles of Organization with the Missouri Secretary of State. The fee is **$50 online or $105 on paper (seen November 2026; confirm current fee)**. You need a registered agent with a Missouri street address. Missouri LLCs **do not file an annual report**, so there is no yearly state filing fee. If you switch from a sole proprietorship to an LLC later, ask the Department of Revenue whether you need to update your sales tax registration (step 4).

## Name

- If you trade under just your own legal name, you do not need to register a name.
- If you use any other name, for example "Jane's Gift Shop", you **must register a fictitious name** with the Secretary of State in step 2.
- An LLC name must include "Limited Liability Company", "Limited Company", "LC", "L.C.", "L.L.C." or "LLC" and must be distinguishable from names already on the Secretary of State's records.

Fictitious names are filed with the state, not the county, so you can check names in one place: the Secretary of State's business entity search. A fictitious name registration does not give you exclusive rights to the name.

## State income tax

**Missouri taxes personal income**, including your profit as a sole proprietor or single-member LLC owner. You report it on your Missouri return (Form MO-1040), and there is no separate registration. The Department of Revenue's 2026 estimated tax form shows rates rising to a top rate of 4.7% (seen November 2026; confirm current rates). You may need to pay Missouri estimated tax if you expect to owe $100 or more. Kansas City and the City of St. Louis each charge a 1% earnings tax on residents, and on business profits earned there (check with each city). Ask an accountant what applies to you. Federal tax is in step 9.

## Consumer privacy law

Missouri does not appear to have a broad consumer privacy law like some other states (checked November 2026; confirm with the Attorney General). Missouri's **data breach notice law applies to any business** that holds Missouri residents' personal information: you must tell affected customers after a breach. See step 11.
