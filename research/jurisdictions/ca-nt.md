## Business structure

- **Sole proprietorship** (recommended for a solo retailer to start). The Corporate Registries page describes a proprietorship as "A business owned by a single individual, where there is no legal distinction between the owner and the business, and the owner is personally liable." Registration of proprietorship business names is under the Partnership and Business Names Act (the page also calls it the Partnership Act). Options listed: proprietorship, partnership, limited partnership, limited liability partnership; incorporation is under the Business Corporations Act. The page notes you can register a business name first and incorporate later. Source: https://www.justice.gov.nt.ca/en/business-names/page/2/ (Entity types tab), overview at https://www.justice.gov.nt.ca/en/business-names/
- Corporate Registries (Legal Registries, Department of Justice) administers this. Contact: corporateregistries@gov.nt.ca, 1-867-767-9304, 1st Floor Stuart M. Hodgson Building, 5009 49th Street, Yellowknife.
- Screenshot candidate for step 1: https://www.justice.gov.nt.ca/en/business-names/page/2/ (Entity types). Note: this is one of several "tabs" implemented as separate paginated URLs (/page/1 to /page/4), so no click is needed if the URL is used directly. The https://www.justice.gov.nt.ca/en/business-registrations/ page is a shorter alternative.

## Name search and registration

- **Who must register:** a business name must be registered if you "use a business name that is not your own name; are engaged in trading, mining, or manufacturing; or form a partnership. You are only exempt from registration if you conduct business using your own, exact legal name." Adding a word such as "Consulting" to your own name still requires registration. Source: https://www.justice.gov.nt.ca/en/business-names/ (and https://www.justice.gov.nt.ca/en/business-registrations/)
- **Name search required:** yes. The registration instructions say an Application for Name Search and Reservation "must be included, together with its prescribed fee of $25.00" if not previously submitted. The forms page labels the reservation form as required. Source: https://www.justice.gov.nt.ca/en/files/business-names/Sole%20Prop/BN%20Reg/BN%20Inst.en.pdf (PDF dated 2019-11-18 on the forms page)
- **Fees (Partnership and Business Names Act fee schedule, schedule PDF dated June 29, 2018 on the forms page, seen 2026-10-17):** name reservation $25; registration where name was reserved $50; registration where name not reserved $75; renewal $50; changes $25 ($50 if change includes a new unreserved name); ceasing to use a name NIL; search $4; certificate of status $20. Source: https://www.justice.gov.nt.ca/en/files/business-names/Fee%20Schedule/Fee%20Schedule%20PBA.en.pdf
  - So a typical first registration is $25 + $50 = $75 total.
- **Renewal period:** "it must be renewed every 4 years in order to remain active", renewal fee $50. An expired registration cannot be renewed; it must be re-registered (new $25 name reservation and $50 registration). Sources: https://www.justice.gov.nt.ca/en/files/business-names/Sole%20Prop/BN%20Renew/BN%20Renewal%20Inst.en.pdf and the BN Inst PDF above.
- **How to file:** paper only. "The original signed forms must be mailed or delivered by hand or mail to the Corporate Registries office; faxes, email, or other electronic delivery will not be accepted." The form is submitted in duplicate; signature must be original, not electronic. The form asks for the owner's residential address (physical address required; it need not be in the NWT), business address, date of first use and nature of business. Payment by Visa, Mastercard, cheque or money order, or cash/debit in person. Source: BN Inst PDF and https://www.justice.gov.nt.ca/en/business-names/page/4/ (Forms tab).
- **Online search** of registered names is free (basic info) via the Corporate Registries Online System, linked from https://www.justice.gov.nt.ca/en/business-names/
- **Time:** Not confirmed. Name reservation validity period: the search result described a 90-day window to register after reservation, but this was not seen on a fetched official page, so treat as Not confirmed.
- Screenshot candidate for step 2: https://www.justice.gov.nt.ca/en/business-names/page/4/ (Forms tab: Sole Proprietorships register / renew / expired). No click needed at that URL. The fee table itself is only in the PDF above.

## Federal ID

CRA Business Number, shared with the other Canadian guides: https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/business-registration.html. (The City of Yellowknife's Start Your Business page lists CRA registration and notes GST/HST registration is needed above the $30,000 small-supplier threshold; threshold is federal.) ## Sales tax

- **GST 5%** in the Northwest Territories, no provincial or territorial sales tax (PST 0%). Source: CRA GST/HST rates page, https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html (page date modified 2025-04-01, seen 2026-10-17).
- Registration threshold ($30,000 small supplier) is federal and the same as in the Alberta guide.

## Licences

- **Territorial: yes, a licence regime exists, but in Yellowknife the City issues it.** The Business Licence Act (R.S.N.W.T. 1988, c.B-4) says "No person shall carry on a business unless he or she is registered or holds a licence issued under this Act and the regulations" (s. 3), and applies to all businesses carried on in the Territories, subject to exceptions (s. 2; listed exceptions include farmers/ranchers/trappers and newspaper publishing). Licences run from date of issue to March 31 (s. 5). Source: https://www.justice.gov.nt.ca/en/files/legislation/business-licence/business-licence.a.pdf (office consolidation, not official text)
- GNWT statement (2026-02-25): "If you are operating a business in the NWT, you must have a valid business licence." Licences are issued April 1 and expire March 31, fees paid annually. MACA issues licences only outside community boundaries and for communities that do not issue their own (Colville Lake, Dettah, Fort Good Hope, Fort Providence, Fort Resolution, Jean Marie River, Kakisa, Kátł'odeeche, Nahanni Butte, Sambaa K'e, Tsiigehtchic, Wrigley, Łutselk'e). "All other NWT communities issue their own business licences", so Yellowknife businesses go to the City. Source: https://www.gov.nt.ca/en/newsroom/nwt-business-licence-renewals-due-april-1-2026
- Territorial fee where MACA issues the licence: $218 for resident or non-resident business (fees regulation, in force April 1, 2025). Source: https://www.justice.gov.nt.ca/en/files/legislation/business-licence/business-licence.r1.pdf. This does not apply to a Yellowknife retailer; the City fee below applies.
- **Yellowknife:** "A business licence is legally required to operate, no matter the type of business." Source: https://www.yellowknife.ca/start-your-business. Governing by-law: Business Licence By-law No. 3451.
- Fee (City of Yellowknife Fees and Charges By-law No. 4436, Part 3, consolidation file dated 2026-06, includes amendments to April 2025): Retail Business $200.00; Home Occupation new application $200.00 plus the development permit fee (non-refundable), renewal $200.00. Source: https://www.yellowknife.ca/sites/default/files/2026-06/CONSOLIDATED%20FEES%20AND%20CHARGES%20BY-LAW%20NO%204436.pdf (seen 2026-10-17). Whether the City licence is annual with an April 1 cycle: Not confirmed on a city page.
- Development permit fee for Home Based Business: $50 per application was reported by search (By-law 5092) and the 2016 form; not verified in the fee by-law text read here, so Not confirmed (older city form also showed $200 + $50 = $250, 2016).

## Municipal (Yellowknife) home-based business

- Process: Business Licence Application and Development Permit Application for Home Based Business, a combined form, submitted by email to businesslicence@yellowknife.ca or in person at City Hall, 4807-52 Street. The form cites Business Licence By-law No. 3451 and Zoning By-law No. 5045. It asks: registered with WSCC?, one address only?, condominium corporation?, employees other than residents?, commercial vehicles?, customers/visitors on site?, goods stored on property and where, hours, and requires property owner acknowledgment if the applicant is not the owner. Source: https://www.yellowknife.ca/business-licence-application-development-permit-application-home-based-business (a PDF) and https://www.yellowknife.ca/business-licences
- Step order on the city page: determine licence type, submit application, check Start Your Business (WSCC registration, inspections, insurance). BizPaL (https://www.bizpal.ca) is recommended for a permit checklist. City page also lists "City Zoning and Development: do I require a Development Permit?".
- Processing time of 10 business days appeared in a search summary only: Not confirmed.
- Zoning rules for home-based business in By-law 5045 were not read: Not confirmed.

Alternative: https://www.yellowknife.ca/start-your-business (nine-step list). ## Consumer protection and privacy

- **Privacy:** the OPC page states "PIPEDA also applies to organizations in the Northwest Territories, Yukon and Nunavut, as they are considered federally-regulated" and that only Alberta, BC and Quebec have substantially similar private-sector laws. So PIPEDA applies to a Yellowknife retailer. Source: https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/r_o_p/prov-pipeda/ (date modified 2020-05-11)
- **Consumer protection:** the Consumer Protection Act (R.S.N.W.T. 1988, c.C-17, consolidation shows amendments to SNWT 2025, c.2) has Part VII Direct Sales Contracts (contract form/content, cancellation). Its table of contents has no internet-sales or distance-contract part, and my search of the text for "internet", "online" and "electronic" found none in that sense. Source: https://www.justice.gov.nt.ca/en/files/legislation/consumer-protection/consumer-protection.a.pdf (office consolidation, not official). MACA Consumer Services administers it (consumer_affairs@gov.nt.ca, 867-767-9161 ext. 21021/21022, per the GNWT statement above). Online sellers should still follow federal Competition Act and general sale of goods rules; not researched here.
