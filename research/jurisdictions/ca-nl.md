## Business structure

- **Sole proprietorship** (recommended for a solo retailer to start). The Commercial Registration Division FAQ defines it as "a business consisting of one owner" who is liable "to the full extent of his or her personal assets"; incorporation is the way to limit liability. Source: https://www.gov.nl.ca/dgsnl/faq/commercial-registration/
- The Registry of Companies incorporates NL for-profit corporations ($300.00) and not-for-profits ($70.00), and registers limited partnerships, condominiums and co-operatives. Same FAQ page (fees as listed on the page, seen 2026-10-17; extra-provincial registration $560.00 with share capital, $260.00 without). Only local incorporations can file online.
- Screenshot candidate for step 1: https://www.gov.nl.ca/dgsnl/registries/companies/corp-about/ (short page; states there is no business name registry law). The FAQ above is the alternative (it has the sole proprietorship answer, but it is a long accordion-style FAQ; items may need a click to expand).

## Name search and registration

- **Who must register:** No one. "Currently, Newfoundland and Labrador does not have legislation governing a Business Name Registry." A sole proprietor therefore has no provincial business-name registration, fee or renewal, and no NUANS search is required by the province. Source: https://www.gov.nl.ca/dgsnl/registries/companies/corp-about/
- **Name approval applies to corporations only.** The Registry of Companies checks name availability within NL only; a name search is by phone (709) 729-3317 or online, and a name can be reserved before incorporating. Source: FAQ above; policy page https://www.gov.nl.ca/gs/?p=1758 (found by search, not fetched).
- **Fee / renewal for a sole-proprietor trade name:** none; not applicable.
- A sole proprietor using a trade name should still check the federal Trademarks Database and domain availability (general advice, not an NL requirement); common-law passing-off and the Competition Act are not reviewed here.
- Screenshot candidate for step 2: https://www.gov.nl.ca/dgsnl/registries/companies/corp-about/ (shows the "no Business Name Registry" statement; no click needed, static page).

## Sales tax

- **HST 15%** in Newfoundland and Labrador (no separate PST). Source: CRA GST/HST rates table, https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html (page date modified 2025-04-01; seen 2026-10-17)
- No separate provincial sales tax account. The $30,000 small-supplier registration threshold is federal and the same as in the Ontario guide (not re-fetched here).

## Licences

- **Provincial general business licence:** none found. No NL page said a general licence is required for a retailer, but no official page was found that says there is none either. Not confirmed. The City points to the provincial Business Navigator Service (one-on-one help on required provincial licences and permits) and to BizPaL (https://www.canada.ca/en/services/business/permits/bizpal.html is not fetched; the City's page describes it). Source: https://www.stjohns.ca/business-development/business-supports/starting-a-business/
- **St. John's (municipal step):**
  - The City's "Apply for a Permit or Licence" list shows no general retail or home-business licence; listed licences are for amusement machines, mobile vending, lodging houses, taxicabs, etc. A retailer selling outdoors from a stand, cart or table needs a Mobile Vending Licence. Source: https://www.stjohns.ca/en/business-investment/business-licenses.aspx
  - **Change of Occupancy Permit: $50** (Permit and Licence Fees page, seen 2026-10-17). Whether a home-based business needs it is not stated on the page. Source: https://www.stjohns.ca/en/building-development/permit-fees-and-rates.aspx
  - **Home occupation rules** (Development Regulations, as amended September 2026, s. 6.22.1): in a dwelling unit it must not exceed the lesser of 45 m2 or 25% of gross floor area; "no open storage or display of goods"; "no wholesale sales. Retail sales may be permitted provided they are incidental to the Home Occupation"; operated by a resident with no more than 2 non-resident employees on site at once; no significant increase in traffic, noise or other nuisance; sufficient parking; no change in type, nature or intensity without Council approval. Source: https://www.stjohns.ca/media/4bajao0x/development-regulations-april-21-2026.pdf (redirects to the City's CDN copy "development-regulations_pdf-september-29-2026.pdf", text read there).
  - A separate **Home Office** (s. 6.23): max 20 m2, office use only, no wholesale or retail sales, no non-resident employees, no client visits (definition). An online-only seller working from a spare room may fit this or home occupation; which one applies is for the City to say.
  - The City's Forms page lists a Planning/Development Application for home occupation and home office, with approval required before starting (reported by search snippet of https://m.stjohns.ca/forms-permits; the page itself did not load, so treat as not directly confirmed). Contacts: Permit Counter 709-576-8565, permits@stjohns.ca; Business Information Centre 709-576-8107, Business@StJohns.ca.
  - Screenshot candidate for the municipal step: https://www.stjohns.ca/business-development/business-supports/starting-a-business/ (static; links to the Starting a Business and Municipal Regulations guides). The older home-based-business page at https://m.stjohns.ca/doing-business/starting-or-operating-business/home-based-business returned empty text via curl and may be script-loaded or retired; if used, it needs a browser. ## Consumer protection and privacy

- **Privacy:** NL has no private-sector privacy law deemed substantially similar to PIPEDA, so PIPEDA applies as in Ontario. NL's Personal Health Information Act covers health information only. Source: OPC, https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/r_o_p/prov-pipeda/ (page lists NL only for the health act; the list of substantially similar private-sector laws was not re-verified beyond that).
- **Consumer protection:** Consumer Protection and Business Practices Act, SNL2009 c. C-31.1 (amended through 2022 c. 28), Part V Division 2 "Distance Sales Contracts". Applies to contracts not entered into in person where the consumer cannot inspect the goods. Before the contract the supplier must disclose prescribed information (s. 29), including the s. 24 information, email address if available, and a detailed description of the goods; for electronic contracts the information must be made available in a way that requires the consumer to access it and lets them retain and print it, and the consumer must get an express opportunity to correct errors and to accept or decline (s. 30). A copy of the contract is due within 15 days (s. 31). The consumer can cancel within 10 days after receiving the copy if s. 30 or the copy requirements were not met, or if goods are not delivered within 30 days of the stated date (s. 32); refund within 15 days (s. 33). Source: https://www.assembly.nl.ca/legislation/sr/statutes/c31-1.htm (seen 2026-10-17). The 10-day and 30-day wording is summarized from a quick read; confirm the full s. 32 text before publishing it.

## Ordered checklist

1. Structure and name
2. Register a business name (not required for a sole proprietor in NL; no registry)
3. CRA Business Number
4. HST (15%)
5. Municipal licence and zoning (St. John's: home occupation rules and Change of Occupancy Permit)
6. Insurance
7. Bank account
8. Bookkeeping and income tax
9. Payment processor
10. Legal pages and privacy
11. Product compliance
12. Supplier account
