## Business structure

- **Natural person operating a sole proprietorship** (recommended for a solo retailer to start). "Such an enterprise does not have a legal existence separate from its owner." The owner takes all profits and losses. ## Name search and registration (Registraire des entreprises du Québec)

- **Who must register:** natural persons who operate a sole proprietorship "under a name that does not include their first and last names", and (regardless of name) those running a tobacco retail outlet or tanning salon. A sole proprietor trading under their own first and last name may register **voluntarily** (it makes the business public; the same obligations then apply). Source (English, last update September 1, 2026): https://www.quebec.ca/en/businesses-and-self-employed-workers/start-entreprise/register-constitute-enterprise/learn-about-registration/about-registration
- On registration the Registrar assigns a Québec enterprise number (NEQ), used to identify the business with the Registraire and other government agencies (same page).
- **Name rules** (English, last update January 14, 2026): the name, and any "other name" (assumed name) used in Québec, must be in French (Charter of the French language); must not contain expressions reserved by law, an immoral/obscene/scandalous idea, a wrong legal form, or falsely suggest a non-profit or public authority. It must not be likely to be confused with, or falsely linked to, another person's name. Any other name used to identify the business in Québec must be declared in the register. Detailed guide IN-531 is French only. Source: https://www.quebec.ca/en/businesses-and-self-employed-workers/start-entreprise/choose-entreprise-name/definition-rules
- **Name search:** no mandatory NUANS-type report was found for a sole proprietor. The register can be searched (French/English interface) at https://www.registreentreprises.gouv.qc.ca/ ; the name page above also mentions requesting a reservation of a name (reservation for legal persons; not confirmed to apply to sole proprietors).
- **Fees** (RE-101-T, "Fees and Terms of Payment in effect as of January 1, 2026", English courtesy translation dated 2026-09; fees are not taxable). Source: https://cdn-contenu.quebec.ca/cdn-contenu/adm/min/emploi-solidarite-sociale/registraire_entreprises/RE-101_REQ_Tous_Tarifs_2026_EN.pdf (linked from https://www.quebec.ca/en/businesses-and-self-employed-workers/registraire-entreprises-fees/all-fees, last update September 28, 2026). Natural person column:
  - Declaration of registration: **$41.00** regular, $61.50 priority.
  - Initial declaration, if filed within 60 days after the registration date: free (regular) for a natural person.
  - **Annual registration fee, as of 2026: $41.00** (2025: $40.00). Not charged in the year after the year of registration (except re-registration after cancellation). Certain enterprises, including sole proprietors who file a Québec tax return, pay it to Revenu Québec with the income tax return (line 438 of the TP-1 per Revenu Québec; the RE-101 says the fee is due by the tax-payment deadline). Late payment: 5% penalty plus 1% per full month late (max 12 months).
  - **Annual updating declaration:** free (regular) for a natural person; priority service $20.50. Filed late: $20.50 regular / $41.00 priority for deadlines after December 30, 2025.
  - Cancellation: not charged on the fee table seen; a search summary says no charge but this was not read on the official page. Not confirmed.
- **Renewal / annual updating declaration:** every registered enterprise must file one each year, even with no changes, starting the year after registration. Sole proprietorships not filing jointly: filing period **January 1 to June 15**. **Joint filing**: a sole proprietor who files a Québec income tax return can tick the box on line 436 of the TP-1-V so the tax return doubles as the declaration, if the register information is correct; then the annual fee is due by April 30 and the declaration by June 15. Failing two consecutive annual declarations can lead to ex officio cancellation of the registration and penal sanctions. Also: changes outside the annual cycle go in a current updating declaration (a search summary said within 30 days; not read on an official page, Not confirmed). Source (English, last update September 29, 2025): https://www.quebec.ca/en/businesses-and-self-employed-workers/updating-information/update-information-enterprise-register/annual-updating-declaration
- **How to file:** online through "My Office" (Mon bureau) at the Registraire des entreprises, with a clicSÉQUR Express or clicSÉQUR Entreprises access code (from a search summary, not read on the page). The service pages note English-language service is "reserved for individuals covered by the exceptions" in the Charter of the French language, so the filing interface is French by default. The step-by-step page for registering a sole proprietorship was not found in English. Revenu Québec's "Register a New Business" service can register for several programs in one go (see Sales tax). Contact page (French): https://www.quebec.ca/gouvernement/ministeres-organismes/registraire-entreprises/coordonnees-structure/coordonnees
- Screenshot candidate for step 2: https://www.quebec.ca/en/businesses-and-self-employed-workers/start-entreprise/register-constitute-enterprise/learn-about-registration/about-registration (the "Who must file a declaration of registration" list is open text; no click needed). Fee table alternative (PDF, opens in browser viewer): the RE-101 URL above.

## Sales tax (GST + QST, both Revenu Québec)

- **Rates:** GST **5%** on the selling price; QST **9.975%** on the selling price excluding the GST. Source (English, Revenu Québec; no page date shown), seen 2026-10-17: https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/businesses/consumption-taxes/gsthst-and-qst/basic-rules-for-applying-the-gsthst-and-qst/
- **Small supplier threshold:** total worldwide taxable supplies (you and associates) not exceeding **$30,000** in a single calendar quarter or the four preceding calendar quarters. Small suppliers need not register or collect. Once exceeded in one quarter, you stop being a small supplier immediately and must collect on the supply that exceeds it. Source: https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/businesses/consumption-taxes/gsthst-and-qst/registering-for-the-gst-and-qst/small-suppliers/
- **Voluntary registration:** allowed even below $30,000; then tax is collected on all taxable supplies, and you can claim input tax credits (ITCs) and input tax refunds (ITRs). **Once registered, the business must stay registered at least one year.** Same page.
- **One registration covers both:** "A business that carries on a commercial activity in Québec and registers for the QST must also register for the GST/HST." Registering with Revenu Québec for the GST and QST is a single application. Same small-supplier page; also https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/businesses/consumption-taxes/gsthst-and-qst/registering-for-the-gst-and-qst/ ("If you are registered for the GST, you are also registered for the HST").
- **Mandatory QST registration regardless of size** if you retail tobacco, fuel or alcoholic beverages, or sell/lease new tires or road vehicles. Not relevant to a general retailer unless selling those. Same registering page.
- **Timing:** QST: before making your first taxable supply other than as a small supplier. GST: before the 30th day following your first taxable sale other than as a small supplier. Source (English): https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/businesses/consumption-taxes/gsthst-and-qst/registering-for-the-gst-and-qst/how-to-register/
- **How to register:** (a) the "Register a New Business" online service if not yet registered with Revenu Québec (covers consumption taxes, source deductions, corporation income tax and clicSÉQUR); (b) the online service for registering a business in "My Account for businesses" (Consumption Taxes section) if already registered; or (c) paper form LM-1-V, Application for Registration. Same how-to-register page. Guide IN-202-V: https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/online-services/forms-and-publications/current-details/in-202-v/ (not read).
- Cross-border sellers and online sales into other provinces (HST destinations, QST on Québec-bound sales) not researched.
- Resale/wholesale exemption certificate: Not confirmed for Québec (not researched).
- Screenshot candidate for the provincial sales tax step: https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/businesses/consumption-taxes/gsthst-and-qst/registering-for-the-gst-and-qst/how-to-register/ (three registration routes listed; page is static text, no click needed). The threshold is on https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/businesses/consumption-taxes/gsthst-and-qst/registering-for-the-gst-and-qst/small-suppliers/ . Note: the URL ".../registering-for-the-gsthst-and-qst/" returns "Page not found"; use the "gst-and-qst" spelling.

## Licences

- **Provincial:** no general provincial retail business licence was found. The quebec.ca "Permits and certification" page (last update May 1, 2023) lists only pesticide-sales declarations in what was read. Sector permits exist (for example tobacco, alcohol, pesticides, regulated trades). Not confirmed on an official page that no general licence exists. https://www.quebec.ca/en/businesses-and-self-employed-workers/permits-and-certification
- **Montréal (municipal step), certificate of occupancy:** an occupancy permit (certificate of occupancy) is required "to conduct a commercial or industrial activity in a building for purposes other than housing, or use part of your home for professional purposes (home office, etc.)". It is attached to the premises and the operator, and a new one is needed if the operator, premises area or activity changes. The city says to check zoning with the borough permit counter before renting or buying. Page used: the City-wide "how-to" page with borough filter set to **Le Plateau-Mont-Royal** (last updated September 24, 2026): https://montreal.ca/en/how-to/get-occupancy-permit-commercial-industrial-or-professional-activity?arrondissement=PMR . Fees on that borough view (taxes included), seen 2026-10-17: **$376** for "any other use"; $972.25 for bar, restaurant, craft brewer, commercial tourist residence, hotel or B&B. Required documents include owner or co-ownership syndicate authorization. Online and in-person application options exist (accordion, collapsed).
  - **Montréal rules vary by borough.** The fee and content shown are for Plateau-Mont-Royal only; without a borough selected the page shows no fee or steps. Other boroughs (for example Côte-des-Neiges–Notre-Dame-de-Grâce, Ville-Marie) have their own permit counters. A search summary quoted $100 for a home-based professional office in another borough but that figure was not read on an official page: Not confirmed. Whether a home-based online retailer with no customers visiting needs the certificate is not stated; confirm with the borough.

## Consumer protection and privacy

- **Privacy:** the Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector ("Private Sector Act", amended by Law 25) applies, overseen by the Commission d'accès à l'information (CAI). The OPC says Quebec's law is deemed substantially similar to PIPEDA, so it generally applies instead of PIPEDA for collection, use and disclosure within the province; PIPEDA still applies to cross-border transfers and federal works. Source (English, date modified 2020-05-11): https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/r_o_p/prov-pipeda/
  - Law 25 requirements for businesses (CAI page, French, fetched 2026-10-17): the person with the highest authority in a business is responsible for protecting personal information and acts as privacy officer unless they delegate in writing; the officer's title and contact details must be published on the website (or otherwise made accessible). Businesses must adopt and publish governance policies and practices (retention/destruction rules, roles, complaint process). Those collecting personal information by technology must publish a plain-language privacy policy. Consent must be requested for each purpose, in clear terms and separately from other information. A privacy impact assessment is required for projects involving information systems or electronic services and before communicating personal information outside Québec. https://www.cai.gouv.qc.ca/protection-renseignements-personnels/sujets-et-domaines-dinteret/principaux-changements-loi-25 (truncated read; the confidentiality-incident and penalty sections were not read there). A search summary cited administrative penalties up to $10M or 2% and penal fines up to $25M or 4% of worldwide revenue: Not confirmed on a page read.
- **Consumer protection (Consumer Protection Act, OPC Québec), distance contracts** (French pages; OPC site states it is simplified and does not replace the law):
  - Before the contract, the merchant must give prescribed information; the consumer can cancel if any is missing or unclear. Full list: https://www.opc.gouv.qc.ca/commercant/pratique-commerce/contrats-distance/renseignement-avant-contrat (found by search, not read).
  - The consumer must be given the chance to review, correct or cancel the order before payment; a written contract (consumer name and address, date, all pre-contract information) must be sent within **15 days**, easy to save and print. Exceptions: perishable goods, auctions, lottery tickets. Source (last modified August 10, 2023): https://www.opc.gouv.qc.ca/commercant/pratique-commerce/contrats-distance/contenu
  - Merchant overview page (fetched 2026-10-17, copyright 2013-2025): https://www.opc.gouv.qc.ca/enligne . Maximum fines under the Act are $87,500 for an individual and $175,000 otherwise, up to 5% of prior-year worldwide revenue in some cases; administrative monetary penalties up to $1,750 (individual) / $3,500 (other), per day of default. The page says it does not cover privacy, which is for the CAI.
  - Charter of the French language applies to the business name and commercial communications (OQLF); not researched beyond the name rule above.

## Ordered checklist

1. Structure and name
2. Register the business with the REQ (needed if the name does not include your first and last names; French name; pay $41, annual updating declaration)
3. CRA Business Number (confirm how it is linked via Revenu Québec)
4. GST and QST (Revenu Québec; 5% + 9.975%; $30,000 small supplier)
5. Municipal licence and zoning (Montréal: certificate of occupancy through the borough)
6. Insurance
7. Bank account
8. Bookkeeping and income tax (Revenu Québec for provincial tax; registry fee on the TP-1)
9. Payment processor
10. Legal pages and privacy (Law 25 privacy policy and officer; OPC distance-contract rules)
11. Product compliance
12. Supplier account
