## Name search and registration

- **Who must register:** "Everyone who forms a partnership, uses a business name other than their own, or indicates more than one member in the business by adding for example, 'and company' after the individual's name, for trading, manufacturing or mining purposes must sign and file a registration within 60 days after forming the partnership or after the business name is first used." Source: https://nunavutlegalregistries.ca/cr_partnerships_en.shtml
- The official guide gives the example: a sole proprietor operating under their own name ("Jean Doe") need not register; "Jean Doe & Company" must. Source: Business Name Package guide, https://nunavutlegalregistries.ca/cr_pdf_en/Partnerships/Packages/PA_Package_DeclarationOfUseOfABusinessName.pdf (undated PDF)
- **Law:** Partnership Act (Nunavut); the form states it is made under section 48 (Form 2, Declaration of Use of a Business Name).
- **Fee:** $50 for a partnership and business name declaration; $25 for a change; $0 (NIL) for a declaration of ceasing to use a name; $0 for searches (each name); $10 certificate of status. Source: Partnership Act Fee Schedule, https://nunavutlegalregistries.ca/cr_pdf_en/Partnerships/Legislation/PA_FeeSchedule.pdf (undated consolidation, seen 2026-10-17)
- **Renewal:** No renewal fee or renewal period appears in the Partnership Act fee schedule or the business-name guide. Not confirmed whether a Nunavut business name registration expires.
- **Name search:** No separate mandatory search step. Registries "review the proposed names to make sure that they aren't confusingly similar to names already on record." Searches are free (fee schedule above) and can be requested by email.
- **How to file:** (1) complete Form 2, giving the full name (initials as first names are not accepted) and a physical address; (2) sign it; (3) phone Legal Registries (867-975-6590) to place a credit card on file for the fee; (4) scan at 300 dpi and email to corporate.registries@gov.nu.ca, or mail or hand deliver the signed original (fax not accepted; cheques payable to Government of Nunavut). Do not put card details in email. Source: guide PDF above. Contact: Legal Registries Division, P.O. Box 1000 Station 570, 4th Floor Building 1106, Iqaluit NU X0A 0H0.
- Screenshot candidate for step 2: https://nunavutlegalregistries.ca/cr_partnerships_en.shtml (the 60-day rule and the Business Name Package link; the fee schedule is a PDF link, no click needed for the text but a click is needed to see fees). The $50 fee is only visible in the PDF https://nunavutlegalregistries.ca/cr_pdf_en/Partnerships/Legislation/PA_FeeSchedule.pdf

## Sales tax

- **GST 5%** in Nunavut, with no territorial sales tax (the CRA table shows 0% provincial/territorial tax). Source: CRA GST/HST rates table, https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html (page date modified 2025-04-01; seen 2026-10-17)
- Registration threshold ($30,000 small supplier) is federal and the same as in the Alberta guide. No territorial seller's permit or tax account turned up.

## Licences

- **Territorial general business licence: yes, one is required.** Nunavut's Business Licence Act (R.S.N.W.T. 1988, c.B-4) is administered by the Consumer Affairs Division of the Department of Community and Government Services. Fee schedule: General Business Licence, Type 1 Resident (operates in Nunavut 8 months or more of each fiscal year) $50.00; Type 2 Non-Resident (under 8 months) $50.00. Source: https://www.gov.nu.ca/sites/default/files/documents/2025-01/CGS_-_Business_Licence_Fee_Schedule_-_ENG.pdf (fetched and read 2026-10-17). The same schedule lists Vendor $60-$120, Direct Seller $30 and Pawnbrokers/Second-Hand Dealers $25, which apply to direct sellers and second-hand dealers, not an ordinary shop.
- Per a summary of the gov.nu.ca page (page itself blocked): licences run per fiscal year, April 1 to March 31; exemptions include businesses licensed under another Act (except the Wildlife Act), farming and trapping, and newspaper/magazine publishing. Consumer Affairs, PO Box 440, Baker Lake NU X0C 0A0, 1-866-223-8139, ConsumerProtection@gov.nu.ca. Source: https://www.gov.nu.ca/en/business-and-entrepreneurship/get-business-licence . Not read first-hand; whether the licence can be applied for online and the renewal date are Not confirmed.
- Screenshot candidate for the territorial licence step: https://www.gov.nu.ca/en/business-and-entrepreneurship/get-business-licence (bot-challenged for non-browser fetch; use a real browser, wait for the challenge to clear).
- **Iqaluit (municipal step):** City of Iqaluit Business Licensing Bylaw No. 859 (effective July 10, 2018 per the City Q&A). The City page states "Anyone conducting business within Iqaluit, either wholly or partially, is required to hold a valid business license," licences "must be renewed annually," applications are online via the CloudPermit portal, and a WSCC (NWT and Nunavut Workers' Safety and Compensation Commission) certificate of compliance is needed or the licence cannot be processed. Source: https://iqaluit.ca/content/apply-business-license. A sole proprietor must provide proof of registration with Nunavut Legal Registries (a Certificate of Business Name) and the WSCC certificate (Bylaw s. 2.14, 2.55). City phone (867) 979-5600 per the page.
  - **Fees (Bylaw Schedule B, resident, new/full year or renewal after Feb 1 / renewal on or before Jan 31):** Class 5 Hobbyist (under 30 worker hours/week) $50 / $25; Class 4 Micro (30 to 90 hours/week) $100 / $50; Class 3 (91-150 hours) $200 / $150. Late-renewal administration fee $50 (after Feb 15). Renewals due Jan 31 (s. 5.8). Fines for operating unlicensed: Class 5 $250 first offence, Class 4 $500. Source: https://iqaluit.ca/sites/default/files/eng_-_business_license_bylaw_no._859.pdf (seen 2026-10-17; fees may have been revised since 2018, confirm on the portal).
  - **Home-based business:** the bylaw defines it as a business run by a resident "as a use secondary to the Residential use of the building and as defined in the current Zoning Bylaw." A home-based business in rented premises needs a Letter of Consent from the owner (s. 5.1(vii)). Older City checklists mention a Home Based Business form and a possible Development Permit; the Zoning Bylaw's home-business rules were not read. Not confirmed.
  - Exempt from the City licence: Inuit carvers/craftspersons/performers selling their art, Inuit hunters selling country food, and government bodies (s. 4.7). A general retailer is not exempt.
  - Screenshot candidate for the municipal step: https://iqaluit.ca/content/apply-business-license . The apply link leads to the CloudPermit portal (click needed, sign-in likely; do not capture past the landing page).

## Consumer protection and privacy

- **Privacy:** Nunavut has no private-sector privacy law substantially similar to PIPEDA (only Alberta, BC and Quebec do), and the OPC states PIPEDA applies in Nunavut (with NWT and Yukon). Source: https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/r_o_p/prov-pipeda/ (date modified 2020-05-11)
- **Consumer protection:** Consumer Protection Act, C.S.Nu. c.C-160 (official consolidation current to June 3, 2025). A keyword read of the full text found no internet-sales or distance-contract provisions. It has Part VI.1 Unfair and Unconscionable Practices (ss. 72.1-72.4) and Part VII Direct Sales Contracts, which applies to sales made away from the vendor's usual place of business after an unsolicited word-of-mouth or telephone approach at the buyer's residence, excludes sales under $100, and says a "recognized retail store" does not include a dwelling or mail order office. Regular online sales are not stated to fall under Part VII. Source: https://www.nunavutlegislation.ca/en/file-download/download/public/8872 . Administered by Consumer Affairs (contact above).

## Ordered checklist

1. Structure and name
2. Register a business name (only if not trading under own name alone; $50, within 60 days)
3. CRA Business Number
4. GST (5%)
5. Territorial general business licence ($50, per fiscal year) and WSCC certificate
6. Municipal licence and zoning (Iqaluit: Bylaw 859, annual renewal by Jan 31)
7. Insurance
8. Bank account
9. Bookkeeping and income tax
10. Payment processor
11. Legal pages and privacy
12. Product compliance
13. Supplier account
