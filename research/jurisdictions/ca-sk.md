## Business structure

- **Sole proprietorship** (recommended for a solo retailer to start). The Corporate Registry describes it as "a business with only one owner. It is the simplest and most flexible business structure." The owner may be an individual or a corporation. The Registry says it "cannot provide advice about whether you should incorporate"; see a lawyer or accountant. Partnership: two or more owners, each potentially liable for all debts. Business corporation: "a legal entity that has a separate legal existence apart from its shareholders and directors." Source: https://www.saskregistries.ca/corporateregistry/registeringabusiness/chooseabusinesstype
- Corporate Registry (Saskatchewan Registry Services, operated by ISC) registers sole proprietorships and partnerships and incorporates corporations. Source: https://www.saskatchewan.ca/business/entrepreneurs-start-or-exit-a-business/saskatchewan-corporate-registry (redirects to saskregistries.ca).
- Note: older isc.ca/CorporateRegistry URLs now return "Page not found"; the live site is saskregistries.ca.

## Name search and registration

- **Who must register:** The Business Names Registration Act s. 4(1): "every person who carries on business under a business name shall cause the business name to be registered". It applies to sole proprietorships, partnerships and limited partnerships (ISC Act summary). Unlike Manitoba and New Brunswick, the Act's definition of "business name" for an individual sole proprietor is "a name or style, including his own family name or surname", so a sole proprietor trading under their own surname is within the definition. Whether a trader using only their plain full legal name must register is not stated on a plain-language official page (Not confirmed). Operating unregistered is an offence: fine up to $1,000 (s. 23). Sources: https://publications.saskatchewan.ca/api/v1/products/399/formats/439/download (unofficial consolidation); https://www.saskregistries.ca/corporateregistry/actsandregulations/the-business-name-registration-act
- **Name search:** No NUANS report is mentioned. Instead a **name reservation** is required first: "You must have a name reservation number prior to registering your business if you wish to use a name." Done online in the Corporate Registry application (File, Business Names, Reserve name, purpose "Saskatchewan business name registration", type "sole proprietor"; "check name" shows whether similar or exact names exist). An available name is held automatically for **90 days**. Choosing the wrong reservation purpose means starting over with another full fee. Source: https://www.saskregistries.ca/corporateregistry/registeringabusiness/searchandreserveabusinessname
- **Fees** (Corporate Registry Fees, "Effective April 15, 2026", seen 2026-10-17). Source: https://www.saskregistries.ca/hubfs/Corporate-Registry-Fees-Table-04-2026.pdf (linked from https://www.saskregistries.ca/fees/corporateregistryfees, where the fee tables do not appear in the page text):
  - Reservation of a business name (for-profit Saskatchewan entity): **$50.00**
  - Registration of a business name: **$65.00**
  - Renewal of a business name: **$60.00**
  - Cancellation of a business name: $15.00
  - Notice of sole proprietor information update / business address change: free
  - So starting a sole proprietorship name costs $50 + $65 = $115 (the fee table lists no sole-proprietor-specific rows; the $65 and $60 rows are the "Business Name" ones).
- **Renewal:** every **three years** after the original registration date; may be filed up to three months before expiry; a renewal must be filed even if nothing changed. If missed, the registration expires, and after 90 days the name is available to others and you must reserve and register again. Changes of address must be reported within **15 days**. Source: https://www.saskregistries.ca/corporateregistry/maintainandamendyourbusinessinformation/maintainingasoleproprietorshipregistration
- **How to file:** online in the Corporate Registry application (create an account first: https://www.saskregistries.ca/corporateregistry/create-a-corporate-registry-online-account). Registration also registers the business with the CRA Business Number program, and the applicant may opt in the same session to register with the Ministry of Finance (the form asks about tobacco, liquor and fuel sales, and anticipated monthly sales) and ask Workers' Compensation Board to contact them. Sources: https://www.saskregistries.ca/corporateregistry/registeringabusiness/registeryourbusiness and https://www.saskregistries.ca/corporateregistry/registeringabusiness/step-3-preparing-to-register-your-business
- Business information is public: profile reports show entity type, status, registration date, nature of business, mailing address, owners and event history. Source: https://www.saskregistries.ca/corporateregistry/findanexistingbusiness/search-find-information-on-an-existing-business

The fee amounts are only in the PDF above, so a fees screenshot needs the PDF, or a click-through if the fees page shows its tables in an accordion (the tables were not in the fetched HTML).

## Sales tax

- **GST 5%** (federal, CRA). Register once past the $30,000 small-supplier limit over four consecutive calendar quarters; voluntary registration allowed. Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html (fetched 2026-10-17)
- **Saskatchewan PST 6%.** "PST is a six per cent sales tax that applies to taxable goods and services... consumed or used in Saskatchewan", including purchases from outside the province; GST is excluded when working out PST. Sources: https://www.saskatchewan.ca/business/taxes-licensing-and-reporting/provincial-taxes-policies-and-bulletins/provincial-sales-tax and Information Bulletin PST-5, Registration and Reporting Requirements (revised July 2025), https://sets.saskatchewan.ca/rptp/wcm/connect/2f7bed69-f481-4fca-8b2d-f80c2acf7d99/PST.005+Registration+and+Reporting+Requirements.pdf?MOD=AJPERES (the sets.saskatchewan.ca host failed certificate verification from this environment, so the PDF was read with certificate checking off; content is a public bulletin)
  - PST is charged on the selling price before GST. For GST-included prices use 5.71% (receipt must show 6%); a combined 11% is allowed if the receipt shows 6% PST + 5% GST. Transaction and payment-processing fees charged to the customer are taxable. Vendors are not required to print their vendor's licence number on invoices (PST-5, section E).
- **Who must register (vendor's licence):** "All businesses operating or making retail sales in Saskatchewan are required to be registered with a PST number, with the exception of businesses qualifying as small traders." Retailers get a **vendor's licence number**; businesses that mostly buy for their own use get a registered consumer number. Out-of-province sellers who make retail sales into Saskatchewan (including online) must also be licensed, and "small trader guidelines do not apply to non-resident vendors." There is no dollar sales threshold for a retailer. (PST-5, section B)
- **Small-trader exemption (narrow):** Individuals who operate from home and make craft-type goods sold on a small or non-commercial basis need not be licensed only if annual sales are under **$10,000**, the goods are produced and sold from their residence, and PST is paid on supplies. It does not extend to sales outside the residence (farmers' markets, online marketplaces) or to commercial customers; small traders that "advertise and compete in the retail market with other licensed vendors" must be licensed. In practice a reseller selling online should assume it must register. (PST-5, section C)
- **Marketplace sellers:** A seller that sells only through a registered marketplace facilitator that collects PST need not be licensed for those sales, but must collect PST on sales through other channels. (PST-5, section B)
- **Main exemptions relevant to a retailer:** PST-5 names basic groceries, reading materials, agricultural equipment and prescription drugs as exempt. Sales shipped or delivered out of the province by the retailer are exempt, as are sales to other vendors for resale (vendor's licence number recorded) and to federal government departments. Detailed taxable/exempt lists are in product-specific bulletins (for example PST-2 and PST-15, found by search, not read); check the bulletin for the products you sell. (PST-5, section E)
- **How to register (SETS):** Online through Saskatchewan eTax Services: complete the "New Business Registration" form to apply for a PST licence (the same form covers LCT, VPT, BCP and IFTA). Businesses already in another tax program use a Service Request to Add account. Or mail/email a registration form to the Ministry of Finance, Revenue Division, PO Box 200, Regina SK S4P 2Z6. The official pages mention no registration fee for the PST licence, but do not say "no fee" either (Not confirmed). Security may be required before a licence is issued ("You may be required to post security"). Sources: https://www.saskatchewan.ca/business/taxes-licensing-and-reporting/provincial-taxes-policies-and-bulletins/provincial-sales-tax/apply-for-a-pst-number and PST-5 section D.
- **Buying inventory for resale exempt:** No separate resale certificate. "Licensed vendors may purchase goods for resale tax free from suppliers by quoting their vendor's licence number at the time of purchase." The supplier must check the PST On-Line Registry (it confirms an active licence but does not show the number) and record the vendor's licence number on the invoice or in its records. The licence does not allow tax-free purchase of items for the business's own use, and goods taken from inventory for own use are self-assessed. (PST-5, sections B and E.) Registry: https://sets.saskatchewan.ca/rptp/portal/footer/pst-registry
- **Filing:** returns are monthly (over $12,000 annual tax remitted), quarterly ($4,800 to $12,000) or annual (under $4,800); electronic returns are due the last day of the month after the period (PST-5, section I; exact due-date wording not reproduced). The licence must be displayed where customers can see it; it is not transferable and does not replace a municipal business licence (PST-5, section E).
- Screenshot candidate for the PST step: https://www.saskatchewan.ca/business/taxes-licensing-and-reporting/provincial-taxes-policies-and-bulletins/provincial-sales-tax/apply-for-a-pst-number (plain page; the "Apply" button leads to SETS, which needs a sign-in for registration; the public PST Registry page is https://sets.saskatchewan.ca/rptp/portal/footer/pst-registry and is script-driven, so use a browser).

## Licences

- **Provincial:** No general provincial business licence turned up on the Government of Saskatchewan Start a Business page (https://www.saskatchewan.ca/business/entrepreneurs-start-or-exit-a-business/start-a-business, which says only that starting up "requires business licensing and permits and business registration"). Not confirmed on an official page that says there is none; BizPaL is the tool the City points to for provincial licence checks.
- **Saskatoon** (municipal step): Bylaw 9746 requires every business to hold a business licence, with two main categories, commercial and home-based. A business run from the owner's principal residence gets a **Home-Based Business Licence**. Source: https://www.saskatoon.ca/business-development/business-licenses/business-licence-categories/home-based-business-licence (fetched 2026-10-17)
  - Fees: new business licence **$135.00**; change of location $135.00; annual renewal **$95.00**. Valid one year; review about 2 to 3 weeks. Apply online (Business Licence Online) or by mail to Business Licence Program, City Hall, 3rd Floor, 222 3rd Avenue North.
  - Standards: one non-resident employee at a time; no more than 8 client or business visits per day (3 at once) and 40 per week, which includes deliveries of merchandise; all activity and storage indoors; at most 20% of gross floor area up to 40 m2. Prohibited home-based uses include "on-site sale of any products, goods or merchandise except sales incidental to a personal service trade". Online sales shipped from home are not addressed on the page; confirm with the Business Licence Program.
  - Commercial licence process (Start a Business page): https://www.saskatoon.ca/business-development/business-licenses/start-business
  - Screenshot candidate for the municipal step: https://www.saskatoon.ca/business-development/business-licenses/business-licence-categories/home-based-business-licence (plain page; fees shown in a table).
- Regina and other municipalities: not reviewed; check locally.

## Consumer protection and privacy

- **Privacy:** The OPC page lists only Alberta, British Columbia and Quebec as having private-sector privacy laws deemed substantially similar to PIPEDA; Saskatchewan is not listed for private-sector law, so PIPEDA applies to a Saskatchewan retailer's commercial collection of personal information (this is an inference from the list; the page does not mention Saskatchewan by name). Source: https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/r_o_p/prov-pipeda/ (date modified 2020-05-11)
- **Consumer protection:** The Consumer Protection and Business Practices Act and its Regulations (C-30.2 Reg 1, effective September 1, 2014) cover internet sales contracts. Before the contract the supplier must disclose the prescribed information and give an express chance to accept or decline and to correct errors (s. 3-6); a copy of the contract must go to the consumer within **15 days** (s. 3-7); if disclosure is missed the consumer may cancel until seven days after receiving a copy (s. 3-8(1)), and if no copy is provided, within 30 days of the contract (s. 3-8(2)). Source: https://publications.saskatchewan.ca//api/v1/products/71437/formats/79510/download (unofficial consolidation, amended to Sask. Reg. 72/2015). Regulator: Financial and Consumer Affairs Authority (FCAA), https://fcaa.gov.sk.ca/. The FCAA internet sales contracts guide (https://fcaa.gov.sk.ca/public/CKeditorUpload/Consumer%20Protection/InternetSalesContracts2015.pdf, found by search, not read) is dated around 2015.

## Ordered checklist

1. Structure and name
2. Reserve and register a business name (Corporate Registry, ISC)
3. CRA Business Number
4. GST (5%)
5. Saskatchewan PST (6%) vendor's licence through SETS
6. Municipal licence and zoning (Saskatoon: Home-Based Business Licence)
7. Insurance
8. Bank account
9. Bookkeeping and income tax
10. Payment processor
11. Legal pages and privacy
12. Product compliance
13. Supplier account (quote the PST vendor's licence number to buy resale stock tax-free)
