# Texas: starting a small retail business (solo seller)

Federal items (EIN, Schedule C/SE, estimated tax, nexus in other states, insurance, banking, FTC/CPSC/FDA) are in `us-federal.md` and are not repeated here.

## Business structure options

- **Sole proprietorship** (recommended to start). No state formation filing. If you trade under any name other than your own legal name, you file an assumed name certificate with the county clerk (see below). Sole proprietorships are not subject to Texas franchise tax (Comptroller publication 98-806).
- **LLC** (single-member or multi-member). Formed by filing a Certificate of Formation, Form 205, with the Texas Secretary of State. **Filing fee $300** (Form 806 fee schedule, Revised 09/26; Form 205 instructions). Online filing through SOSPortal (formerly SOSDirect) adds a card convenience fee of $0.25 + 2.25%.
  - **Yearly cost**: Texas LLCs do not file an annual report with the SOS. Instead an LLC is a taxable entity for franchise tax and must file a franchise tax report or, at or below the no-tax-due threshold, an information report plus a Public Information Report (PIR, Form 05-102) with the Comptroller each year by May 15. There is no state filing fee for the PIR. Franchise tax is only owed when total revenue exceeds the no-tax-due threshold ($2,650,000 for 2026–2027 reports).
  - A registered agent with a physical Texas street address is required (the LLC cannot be its own agent).
  - Moving from a sole proprietorship to an LLC later counts as a change of ownership for the sales tax permit, so a new permit is needed.
- **Recommendation**: start as a sole proprietorship. Texas has no state income tax, so the main differences are liability, the $300 filing fee, and the yearly Comptroller reporting for an LLC. Ask an accountant or lawyer if you want liability protection.

## Name search and registration

- **Sole proprietor**: file an assumed name certificate with the county clerk in each county where you keep a business office (or, with no Texas office, where you do business). Fees are set by each county: contact the county clerk. An assumed name gives no exclusive right to the name. County clerk directory: SOS elections page.
- **LLC**: name must be distinguishable from other names on SOS records and include an organizational designation. Exact required words (for example "Limited Liability Company", "LLC") are in Business Organizations Code Sec. 5.056: **Not confirmed** (statute site would not load). SOSPortal name search costs $1 per search; preliminary name check by phone (512) 463-5555. Name reservation lasts 120 days (fee not used in the guide).
- **LLC trading under another name**: files assumed name certificate Form 503 with the SOS, **$25**, valid up to 10 years. Since HB 3609 (Sept 1, 2019) entities file only with the SOS, not the county.

## Federal IDs (brief)

EIN from the IRS (free) for an LLC or anyone hiring; see `us-federal.md`. The Texas sales tax permit can be applied for without an EIN (sole owner uses SSN).

## Sales tax

- **Permit**: Texas Sales and Use Tax Permit, issued by the Texas Comptroller of Public Accounts. **No fee**, but a security bond may be required (amount Not confirmed). Apply online through eSystems ("Texas Online Tax Registration Application"); allow 2–3 weeks. Applicant must be 18+. Sole owner needs SSN and NAICS code; without an SSN use Form AP-201. One permit per place of business; post it at the place of business. Permit holders' phone numbers are public information.
- **Rate**: state **6.25%**. Cities, counties, special purpose districts and transit authorities can add up to 2%, for a maximum combined **8.25%**. Comptroller Sales Tax Rate Locator gives the rate for an address.
- **Filing**: monthly, quarterly or yearly, assigned by the Comptroller. Due the 20th of the month after the period (quarterly: Apr 20, Jul 20, Oct 20, Jan 20; yearly: Jan 20); moves to the next business day on weekends/holidays. File even with no sales. 0.5% timely filing discount. Late penalties: $50 per late report; 5% if 1–30 days late, 10% if over 30 days.
- **Resale certificate**: Form 01-339, Texas Sales and Use Tax Resale Certificate. Blanket certificate allowed. Keep 4 years; a permit number alone is not a substitute. Misuse carries criminal penalties.
- **Remote sellers**: out-of-state sellers with total Texas revenue of **$500,000** or more in the preceding twelve calendar months must get a permit (revenue includes nontaxable sales and sales for resale), by the first day of the fourth month after crossing. Remote sellers may elect a single local use tax rate of 1.75%. Sellers selling only through a marketplace provider that collects do not need a permit.

## US state taxes and fees on the business

- **State income tax**: none. Texas has no personal income tax (Comptroller Fiscal Notes small business page).
- **Franchise tax** (Comptroller): applies to LLCs and corporations, not to sole proprietorships or general partnerships of natural persons. Report due **May 15** each year (next business day if weekend/holiday); first report due May 15 of the year after the entity becomes subject. For 2026–2027 reports: no-tax-due threshold **$2,650,000**; rate 0.375% retail/wholesale, 0.75% other; EZ computation 0.331% (revenue up to $20 million). At or below the threshold: no tax and no franchise tax report, but an information report is still required (since 2024; the No Tax Due Report Form 05-163 is discontinued). $50 late filing penalty.
- **Public Information Report (PIR)**, Form 05-102: LLCs and corporations file each year with the franchise report, due May 15, even below the threshold. Contents are public. Failing to file can forfeit the right to transact business.
- **Annual report with SOS**: none for LLCs.
- **Scam alert**: Comptroller warns of texts claiming a franchise tax or PIR report is past due.

## Local licenses and permits

- **No general state business license.** The Governor's Business Permit Office says the SOS certificate of formation or county assumed name certificate satisfies the "general business license" role. Specific activities may need state licenses: see the Texas Business Licenses & Permits Guide (2026–2027).
- Local (city and county) requirements vary: home occupation and zoning rules, certificates of occupancy, health permits for food, signs. Contact the city and county. Rules not confirmed for any particular city.

## Registered agent and address privacy

- LLC must have a registered agent: a Texas resident or registered entity, with a physical Texas street address (not only a mailbox service). You can be your own agent, but your street address becomes public.
- The initial mailing address can be a PO box.
- PIR officer/manager information is public.
- Sales tax permit holders' phone numbers are public.

## Employer registrations (brief)

Texas Workforce Commission for state unemployment tax when hiring. Workers' compensation is optional for most private employers in Texas (not verified on an official page this session: Not confirmed). See shared insurance step.

## Consumer privacy

Texas Data Privacy and Security Act (TDPSA), effective July 1, 2024 (Attorney General). Applies to those conducting business in Texas or producing products consumed by Texans who process or sell personal data. No revenue threshold; small businesses as defined by the SBA are generally exempt, but a small business must get consent before selling sensitive data.
