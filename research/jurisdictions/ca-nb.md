## Business structure

- **Sole proprietorship** (recommended for a solo retailer to start). "A sole proprietorship business is not required to register, but partnerships and corporations are required to do so." Legal structure is listed as one of the decisions to make before getting a Business Number. Source: https://www2.gnb.ca/content/gnb/en/corporate/promo/farming-new-entrants/farming-register.html (a GNB page written for farms; the rule it states is general)
- Corporate Registry (SNB) registers business names used by sole proprietorships and partnerships and incorporates NB corporations. For choosing a structure it points to the joint provincial/federal **Business Structures Wizard**. Source: https://www2.snb.ca/content/snb/en/sites/corporate-registry.html
- Screenshot candidate for step 1: the Corporate Registry landing page above (shows "Register a business name" and the structure FAQ).

## Name search and registration

- **Who must register:** "Every person other than a partnership who carries on a trading, mining or manufacturing business in New Brunswick and uses a business name other than his/her own name is required to register their business name." A sole proprietor trading under their own name does not need to register. Source: https://www2.gnb.ca/content/gnb/en/services/services_renderer.201450.Register__Renewal_of_a_Business_Name.html (Description tab; content loads by script, so use a browser for the screenshot)
- **Law:** Partnerships and Business Names Registration Act, https://laws.gnb.ca/en/document/cs/P-5
- **When:** Form 5, Certificate of Business Name, must be filed "within two months of commencing business". Source: Procedural Requirements to Register a Business Name, https://www.pxw1.snb.ca/snb7001/b/1000/CSS-FOL-SNB-45-0003B.pdf (bilingual; the English-only URL returned 404)
- **Name search:** required. "Before registration, you must select the proposed business name and obtain a New Brunswick based NUANS name search report." The report comes from a private-sector name search firm and must be no more than 90 days old when the Registry receives it; online filers attach it to the application. Sources: the procedural PDF above, and Choosing a Name in New Brunswick, https://www.pxw1.snb.ca/snb7001/e/1000/CSS-FOL-SNB-45-0168E.pdf
- **Fees** (Fees tab of the service page above; fees exclude HST; effective date shown is 1991-01-01):
  - Certificate of Business Name: **$112** (includes $12 Royal Gazette fee; the procedural PDF splits it as $100 registration + $12 publication)
  - Certificate of renewal of business name: **$62** (includes $12 Royal Gazette fee)
  - Certificate of cessation: $12
  - Appointment or change of agent for service: $12 on the fee table; the procedural PDF says no additional fee when filed with the registration. A non-resident registrant must appoint an agent (Form 7).
- **Renewal:** every **5 years** from the date of registration. Missing it "constitutes an offence under the Act and may result in the cancellation" of the registration. Renewal can be filed online. Source: procedural PDF above.
- **How to file:** online through the Corporate Registry's Online Filing Centre ("Register a business name", "Renew a business name"), https://www2.snb.ca/content/snb/en/sites/corporate-registry.html. Paper Form 5: https://www.pxw1.snb.ca/snb7001/e/1000/CSS-FOL-45-3502E.pdf (found by search, not fetched). Registry contact: Corporate Registry Operations, CR-RC@snb.ca, 435 Brookside Drive, Suite 30, Fredericton.
- Screenshot candidate for step 2: the Register / Renewal of a Business Name service page above (Fees tab shows the $112 and $62 rows).

## Federal ID

CRA Business Number, shared with the other Canadian guides: https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/business-registration.html. GNB notes a sole proprietor's BN cannot be transferred if the structure later changes (farm page above).

## Sales tax

- **HST 15%** in New Brunswick, unchanged since October 1, 2016. Source: CRA GST/HST rates table, https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html (page date modified 2025-04-01)
- No separate provincial sales tax account. Registration threshold ($30,000 small supplier) is federal and the same as in the Ontario guide.

## Licences

- No provincial general business licence turned up on GNB or SNB pages. Not confirmed on an official page that says so.
- **Fredericton** (municipal step): "A home occupation is permitted in any zone that permits a single detached dwelling or mini-home." Rules are in Zoning By-law Z-5, section 7.3(1). Source: https://www.fredericton.ca/business-development/planning-development/zoning-land-use
  - Section 7.3(1) limits a home occupation to a single detached dwelling or mini-home, 1 per residence, 1 non-resident employee, 1 client at a time, owner must live there, no outside sign except a fascia sign, no outside storage. Its prohibited uses include "Retail sale of goods not produced or manufactured on the premises". Source: https://www.fredericton.ca/sites/default/files/2023-04/zoningbylawz5.pdf (file dated April 2023; the city says the by-law is amended often and zoning must be verified with its Community Planning Division)
  - For the guide: a home-based reseller in Fredericton should confirm with Community Planning before starting; whether online-only sales shipped from home count as prohibited "retail sale" is not stated.
  - Screenshot candidate for the municipal step: the zoning-land-use page above (FAQ "Can I have a home based business in my residence?").
- Moncton and Saint John: not reviewed. Moncton's rules are in Zoning By-law Z-222 (https://www5.moncton.ca/docs/bylaws/By-Law_Z-222_Zoning_Arrete_zonage.pdf, found by search, not read).
- Other municipalities: check locally.

## Consumer protection and privacy

- **Privacy:** New Brunswick has no private-sector privacy law deemed substantially similar to PIPEDA (only Alberta, BC and Quebec do), so PIPEDA applies as in Ontario. NB's Personal Health Information Privacy and Access Act covers health information only. Source: OPC, https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/r_o_p/prov-pipeda/ (date modified 2020-05-11)
- **Consumer protection:** Consumer Protection Act, SNB 2024, c.1, has an Internet Sales Contracts division. Before the contract, the supplier must give an express opportunity to accept or decline and correct errors (s. 30(1)(b)); the contract must contain prescribed information (s. 30(2)); a copy must go to the consumer within 15 days (s. 30(3)); consumers can cancel if these are not met (s. 31). Source: https://laws.gnb.ca/en/document/cs/2024,%20c.1 (shown as "Current to 7 June 2024"). FCNB administers it: https://fcnb.ca/en/consumer-protections
