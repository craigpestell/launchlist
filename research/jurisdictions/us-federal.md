## Federal IDs: the EIN

- **What it is:** the Employer Identification Number, the business's federal tax ID, issued by the IRS.
- **Who needs one:** you need an EIN if you have employees, operate as a corporation or partnership, file employment, excise or alcohol, tobacco and firearms tax returns, or (among other cases) operate an LLC. A sole proprietor with no employees can usually use their SSN for federal purposes, but can still get an EIN for banking or state tax registration. (IRS EIN page.)
- **How:** apply online, free, and get the number immediately. The application must be finished in one session and **expires after 15 minutes of inactivity**. You need the responsible party's SSN or ITIN. **Limit of one EIN per responsible party per day.** Form an LLC with the state before applying. (IRS "Get an EIN".)
- **Online hours (Eastern time):** Monday–Friday 6:00 a.m. to 1:00 a.m. the next day; Saturday 6:00 a.m. to 9:00 p.m.; Sunday 6:00 p.m. to midnight. (IRS "Get an EIN", seen 2026-11-26.)
- **Fee:** none. The IRS says you never have to pay a fee for an EIN and warns against websites that charge. SBA repeats this.
- **Alternatives:** fax Form SS-4 to 855-641-6935 (about 4 business days) or mail it to IRS, Attn: EIN Operation, Cincinnati, OH 45999 (about 4 weeks).
- **Name rules on the form:** only letters, numbers, hyphens and ampersands; street address up to 35 characters.
- **Keep:** the EIN confirmation notice (CP 575) shown at the end of the online application. An EIN can't be cancelled, only deactivated (SBA).

## Federal income tax for a sole proprietor

- Report business profit on **Form 1040 with Schedule C**, and self-employment tax on **Schedule SE**. Publications 334 and 583 are the IRS guides. (IRS sole proprietorships.)
- **Self-employment tax:** 15.3% (12.4% Social Security plus 2.9% Medicare). The Social Security part applies to the first $184,500 of combined earnings for 2026. An additional 0.9% Medicare tax applies above $200,000 (single) or $250,000 (married filing jointly). It applies once net self-employment earnings are $400 or more. You can deduct the employer-equivalent half. (IRS self-employment tax page, seen 2026-11-26.)
- **Estimated tax:** required if you expect to owe **$1,000 or more** when you file. Paid with Form 1040-ES, through IRS Online Account or Direct Pay. Due dates: April 15 (income Jan 1–Mar 31), June 15 (Apr 1–May 31), September 15 (Jun 1–Aug 31), January 15 of the next year (Sep 1–Dec 31). Safe harbor: pay at least 90% of this year's tax or 100% of last year's. (IRS estimated taxes.)
- **LLC default tax treatment:** a single-member LLC is a "disregarded entity" taxed on the owner's return (Schedule C) unless it elects otherwise on Form 8832; a multi-member LLC defaults to a partnership. A single-member LLC is still treated as a separate entity for employment and excise taxes. (IRS LLC page.)
- **Form 1099-K:** payment apps and online marketplaces must report when your payments for goods or services exceed **$20,000 in more than 200 transactions**, but they may send one for less. **All income must be reported** whatever the amount. (IRS "Understanding your Form 1099-K", seen 2026-11-26.)
- **Records:** keep records generally **3 years** from filing; 6 years if you under-report income by more than 25%; 7 years for a bad-debt or worthless-securities claim; employment tax records at least 4 years; indefinitely if no return was filed or it was fraudulent. (IRS "How long should I keep records?")
- State income tax: varies by state. Not covered here.

## FinCEN beneficial ownership (BOI)

- Checked 2026-11-26. FinCEN's alert (updated August 11, 2026) says a final rule exempts all entities created in the United States (domestic reporting companies) and their beneficial owners from BOI reporting. The final rule took effect August 14, 2026 and made the March 26, 2025 interim final rule permanent. Only entities formed under foreign law and registered to do business in a state must report. (fincen.gov/boi.)
- Effect on the guide: a US-formed LLC does **not** file a BOI report. Mention it only to warn readers about letters or sites that say otherwise.

## Selling into other states (sales tax nexus)

- **No federal sales tax.** Sales tax is set by each state (and often local governments). Five states have no statewide sales tax; this is left to state files.
- **Economic nexus:** since the Supreme Court's decision in *South Dakota v. Wayfair* (June 21, 2018), states can require out-of-state sellers to register and collect once their sales into the state pass a threshold, even with no physical presence. (Streamlined Sales Tax remote seller pages.)
- **Thresholds:** set by each state. The SST remote seller chart (dated January 11, 2023, so possibly stale) lists, for example, $100,000 for South Dakota and $500,000 for Texas, and notes many states have dropped their 200-transaction tests. **Not confirmed as current for every state**: the guide tells readers to check each state's revenue site rather than quoting a table.
- **Marketplace facilitator laws:** many states require marketplaces (Amazon, Etsy and similar) to collect and remit tax on third-party sales made through them. Sellers may still need to register for their own-site sales, and rules differ by state. (SST marketplace facilitator page.)
- **Streamlined Sales Tax Registration System (SSTRS):** one free online registration covers any or all **24 member states** (23 full members plus Tennessee as associate). Full members: Arkansas, Georgia, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Kentucky, Michigan, Minnesota, Nebraska, Nevada, New Jersey, North Carolina, North Dakota, Ohio, Oklahoma, Rhode Island, South Dakota, Utah, Vermont, Washington, West Virginia, Wisconsin, Wyoming. California, Texas, Florida and New York are **not** members. Returns are still filed with each state, including zero returns. A state may charge if registration is otherwise legally required. (streamlinedsalestax.org, seen 2026-11-26.)
- **Certified Service Providers:** free to remote sellers who have economic nexus only (no physical presence) in member states. Listed CSPs: Avalara, TaxCloud, Sovos, AccurateTax, Avior. (SST.)

## Resale certificates

- **Streamlined Sales and Use Tax Exemption Certificate:** accepted by all 24 SST member states. Give it to the supplier and keep a copy; don't send it to the states. A state may require its own ID number on it. (SST exemptions page.)
- **MTC Uniform Sales & Use Tax Resale Certificate – Multijurisdiction:** the Multistate Tax Commission says 36 states have indicated it can be used as a resale certificate. Updated October 14, 2022. The certificate lists the states and limits. Ask the state's revenue department whether it is accepted. (mtc.gov.)
- Your home state usually has its own resale certificate; covered in state files.

## FTC rules for online sellers

- **Mail, Internet, or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule:** you must have a reasonable basis for any shipping time you state; if you state none, you must ship within **30 days** (50 days if the customer applies for credit with you). If you can't ship on time, you must ask the customer to consent to the delay or cancel and promptly refund. The clock starts when you receive a properly completed order. (FTC business guide.)
- **Endorsement Guides:** endorsements must reflect honest opinions; a material connection (payment, free product, family or employee) must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously; results claims need proof of typical results. (FTC "What People Are Asking".)
- **CAN-SPAM Act:** covers all commercial email, including business-to-business. No false headers or deceptive subject lines; identify the message as an ad; include a **valid physical postal address** (a street address, a registered PO box, or a registered private mailbox); give a clear way to opt out and honor it. Penalties up to **$53,088 per email** (seen 2026-11-26). Opt-outs must be honored within 10 business days (from the same guide, below the excerpt captured; **Not confirmed** on re-read, so the guide says "promptly").
- **Made in USA:** an unqualified "Made in USA" claim means "all or virtually all" made in the US. The Made in USA Labeling Rule (16 CFR Part 323, August 2021) adds civil penalties for false labels, including online. Most products don't have to be labeled with origin, but textile, wool and fur products and cars must disclose it. (FTC "Complying with the Made in USA Standard".)
- **Textile, Wool and Fur Acts:** most textile and wool products need a label with fiber content, country of origin and the manufacturer or dealer (name or RN number). Care labels are a separate FTC rule. Mail-order and online ads must disclose origin. (FTC "Threading Your Way".)
- Children's privacy (COPPA) applies only to sites directed at children under 13. Not researched further; mentioned only in passing.

## Product safety and regulated products

- **CPSC general use products:** manufacturers and importers of products covered by a consumer product safety rule must certify compliance in a **General Certificate of Conformity (GCC)**, based on a test of each product or a reasonable testing program. Any lab can test non-children's products. (CPSC.)
- **CPSC children's products (CPSIA):** nearly all children's products must be tested by a **CPSC-accepted third-party lab** and certified in a **Children's Product Certificate (CPC)**; tracking labels are required. Responsibility sits with the domestic manufacturer or importer. Small Batch Manufacturers can register for relief from some third-party testing but must still issue a CPC. (CPSC.)
- **eFiling:** from **July 8, 2026**, importers of most regulated consumer products must eFile certificates of compliance with CBP. (CPSC banner on testing pages.) Relevant only if you import directly.
- **Retailer role:** retailers who buy from a US manufacturer or importer aren't the certifier, but should ask for the certificate. Importing yourself makes you the importer.
- **Cosmetics (MoCRA):** facilities that manufacture or process cosmetics must register with FDA and renew **every two years**; the "responsible person" (the manufacturer, packer or distributor named on the label) must list products and report serious adverse events. FDA does not issue compliance certificates for registration (notice of September 9, 2026). Small-business exemptions exist; their thresholds are **Not confirmed** here. (FDA MoCRA pages.)
- **Food:** facilities that manufacture, process, pack or hold food for US consumption must register with FDA and renew every other year (Bioterrorism Act, FSMA). Retail food establishments have exemptions; state and local health rules also apply. **Not confirmed** for a reseller of packaged food; guide says to check. (FDA food facility registration.)

## Insurance

- SBA lists six common types: general liability, product liability, professional liability, commercial property, home-based business insurance (a rider on homeowner's insurance) and a business owner's policy (BOP). It says businesses with employees must carry workers' compensation, unemployment and disability insurance, and that requirements vary by state. (SBA "Get business insurance".)
- No federal requirement for a solo retailer with no employees. Not confirmed per state; left to state steps.

## Banking and payments

- SBA: open a business bank account as soon as you start taking or spending money as the business. Banks commonly ask for an EIN (or SSN for a sole proprietor), formation documents, ownership agreements and a business license. A payment processor still needs a business checking account to pay out to. (SBA "Open a business bank account".)
- Merchant account fees to compare (SBA): discount rate, transaction fees, AVS fees, batch fees, monthly minimums.
