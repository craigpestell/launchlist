## Business structure

- **Sole proprietorship** (recommended for a solo retailer to start): "a business owned by only 1 person. It's not incorporated." Partnerships (2 or more partners, not incorporated) and companies (separate legal entity) are the other options. NS registers a sole proprietorship through the partnership system, with one partner listed. The page advises discussing the choice with legal and accounting advisors. Source: https://www.novascotia.ca/choose-legal-structure-your-business-or-non-profit
- Screenshot candidate for step 1: https://www.novascotia.ca/choose-legal-structure-your-business-or-non-profit (static page, no click needed).

## Name search and registration

- **Who must register:** "Most sole proprietorships need to register with Registry of Joint Stock Companies to operate in Nova Scotia." Exempt: sole proprietors using only their personal name (for example, "Jane Doe" need not be registered but "Jane Doe Consulting" must be), sole proprietorships registered in New Brunswick, and those whose only purpose is farming or fishing. Sources: https://www.novascotia.ca/register-sole-proprietorship and https://www.novascotia.ca/choose-legal-structure-your-business-or-non-profit
- **Name reservation first (required before registering):** RJSC must approve the name. Approval gives **90 days** to register; after that the reservation expires and the fee is payable again. The reservation search includes a **Nuans report** (Atlantic region search if you only plan to trade in NS; federal search if beyond the Atlantic region or the name contains "Canada"/"Canadian"). Page notes RJSC "is currently experiencing processing delays". Source: https://www.novascotia.ca/reserve-name-your-business-or-non-profit
  - Reservation fees (seen 2026-10-17): Atlantic search **$60.52**; Atlantic search with your own Nuans report $15.12; federal search $75.58; federal with your own Nuans report $15.12 (own report must be dated within 90 days). Same source.
- **Registration fee:** **$68.55** (sole proprietorship). Seen 2026-10-17 at https://www.novascotia.ca/register-sole-proprietorship
- **Renewal:** **annual**, **$68.55**. The page gives no fixed date; a notice is sent by mail or email, but renewing on time is the owner's responsibility. Sole proprietors must also file an annual return. Sources: https://www.novascotia.ca/register-sole-proprietorship and https://www.novascotia.ca/sole-proprietorship-registry-joint-stock-companies-requirements (no amounts on the second page; it points to a Schedule of Fees, not fetched).
- **How to file:** online (RJSC account, Sole Proprietor Registration Form, signed PDFs uploaded), by mail with the paper form, or in person at RJSC or an Access Nova Scotia centre. Payment: credit card (online requires a credit card), debit, cheque or money order payable to the Minister of Finance, cash.
- **Time:** "1 to 2 weeks to get the Certificate of Registration and Business Number" per the registration page. Name reservation time: see the reserve page (processing delays noted; no figure confirmed).
- A recognized agent is not required for a sole proprietor living in NS. Information filed with RJSC is public record.

## Sales tax

- **HST 14%** in Nova Scotia effective **April 1, 2025**, down from 15% (15% applied October 1, 2016 to March 31, 2025). The CRA page states: "As of April 1, 2025, the HST rate has decreased to 14% in Nova Scotia." Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html (page date modified 2025-04-01, seen 2026-10-17). The federal/provincial split of the 14% was not read on the page.
- No separate provincial sales tax account. Registration threshold ($30,000 small supplier) is federal and the same as the Ontario guide.

## Licences

- **Provincial general business licence:** none found on the RJSC or NS business pages. Not confirmed on an official page that says so.
- **Halifax (municipal step):** HRM has no single home-occupation rule; each plan area has its own Land Use By-law (LUB). A **Development Permit - basic and land use** is required for a home-based business with no renovations. Rules on the HRM page: the business must be owner-occupied (your primary residence); the basic permit applies only if the space is already configured, needs no alteration, and is **under 20 m2**; the rules also apply to an accessory building; the application needs the business name, number of employees/clients and nature of the business, a site plan and a floor plan; a landlord letter is needed for a rental unit; a sign needs a separate sign permit. Applied for online through the HRM customer portal. Renovations need a building permit instead. Source: https://www.halifax.ca/home-property/building-development-permits/house-home-permits/home-improvement-permits (fetched with curl; WebFetch returned 403)
  - The fee for the home-based business development permit was not found on that page (it refers to the Permit Fees page). Not confirmed.
  - Zoning varies by area. Example only, from search excerpts and not read in full: the Halifax Mainland LUB limits a home occupation to one per lot, one storey, 50% of floor area to 400 sq ft, and residents only. Check the LUB for the property's zone via HRM's interactive property map.
  - HRM vending/artisan licences (by-law C-501) apply only to specific sites on municipal land, not to private-property or online sales. Source: https://www.halifax.ca/business/licences/how-get-a-vendors-licence/artisan-licence (from search summary, not fetched).
  - Screenshot candidate for the municipal step: the HRM home-improvement-permits URL above. The page has tabs (Home Renovations, Deck, ... Home Based Business); **click the "Home Based Business" tab** to reveal the content.
- Other municipalities: check locally.

## Consumer protection and privacy

- **Privacy:** Only Alberta, BC and Quebec have general private-sector laws deemed substantially similar to PIPEDA. Nova Scotia appears only for health information (Personal Health Information Act). So PIPEDA applies to a Nova Scotia retailer's commercial activities. The OPC page does not say this in so many words for NS; it is an inference from the list. Source: https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/r_o_p/prov-pipeda/ (date modified 2020-05-11)
- **Consumer protection:** Consumer Protection Act, R.S.N.S. 1989, c. 92, internet sales provisions (ss. 21W to 21AF), and the Internet Sales Contract Regulations, N.S. Reg. 91/2002 (in effect Dec. 19, 2003). Before the contract the supplier must disclose (s. 3): name and business address, contact details, a description of the goods, itemized prices including taxes and shipping, total, currency, payment terms, delivery date and arrangements, and cancellation/return/refund policies. The Act requires a copy of the contract within 15 days. Provisions do not apply where total payable is under $50 (s. 2). Cancellation rights are in s. 6. Sources: https://novascotia.ca/just/regulations/regs/cpintrnt.htm (unofficial consolidation, copyright 2009, no as-of date) and the Act, https://nslegislature.ca/sites/default/files/legc/statutes/consumer%20protection.pdf (found by search, not read). Details of the cancellation periods (7 days / 30 days) come from search summaries of the regulation and were not read directly.

## Ordered checklist

1. Structure and name
2. Reserve a name (Nuans), then register the sole proprietorship with RJSC (not needed under your own personal name)
3. CRA Business Number
4. HST (14%)
5. Municipal permit and zoning (Halifax: Development Permit for a home-based business)
6. Insurance
7. Bank account
8. Bookkeeping and income tax
9. Payment processor
10. Legal pages and privacy
11. Product compliance
12. Supplier account
